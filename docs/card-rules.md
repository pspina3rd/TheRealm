# Card rules for the play-throughs — draft for Phil, 20 October

Step 1 of running the test properly (agreed 19 October): what each card means
for a question, and what counts as a play, written **before** any run. Each
rule is tagged and cited. **[C]** rules are mine and wait on Phil. Nothing
here goes into the record.

## What is a play

1. **[P]** Nine plays a call. Asking her something, asking a coworker, or
   looking something up each uses one. (`settled.md` §2, *Nine plays*)
2. **[P]** The opening is free: the greeting and the universal question.
   (§2)
3. **[P]** Identification is two plays: ask her for something searchable,
   then search on it. (§2)
4. **[P·C]** The aside — asking a coworker over your shoulder while she is
   on the line — is a play. She hears it. (§2, *The aside*; Phil, 19
   October)
5. **[P, 20 October]** Offering a remedy or a fix is a play. What the agent says to
   explain the fix inside the same turn is part of that play, not another.
6. **[P, 20 October]** Next steps cost a play. The goodbye is free.

## Suits — what the question asks for

7. **[P]** Who · What · When · Where · Why · How. (§2, *The six suits*)
8. **[P, 20 October]** The suit is the kind of answer the question asks
   for — a person, a thing or action, a time, a place, a reason, a way of
   doing it — not the question's first word.

## Angles — how the question asks it

9. **[P]** Verify · Validate · Expand · Contract are the four angles;
   Random is the player not choosing. (§2)
10. **[P]** A statement is an inquiry: it invites a yes or a no. (§2, *A
    statement is an inquiry*)
11. **[C·ok, 20 October]** **Validate** checks with her, two ways:
    - **A guess at something she has not said** tests a fact: yes confirms
      it, no corrects it and hands over the right thing.
    - **A paraphrase of what she has said** confirms nothing about the fact
      (Phil, tentative, §2 *Her side, mirrored*), but shows she was heard —
      *paraphrase for comprehension, then act* is one of the soft skills
      that are the mechanic (`docs/handover/memory/therealm-design-frame.md`).
      It costs a play, and her sheet decides what it is worth: repetition
      *high* is settled by it, *low* reads it as not listening, empathy
      *high* can tell whether it was meant.
12. **[C·ok, tentative, 20 October]** **Verify** checks against a source.
    - **At the record:** checks something she said against what TLC holds —
      a lookup, so a play.
    - **At her:** asks her to go and check something she has — her memory,
      her account page, the bag. What comes back can be right or wrong, sure
      or unsure, and neither tells the player the other (Phil, 12 October).
    - Validate: the agent says it, she answers yes or no. Verify: the agent
      asks, she goes and looks.
    - Phil: *"I think so. It's not terribly clear to me, though it seems
      accurate."*
    - **Framing, Phil, 20 October:** not *"So the bag's been set so only you
      can open it?"* but *"Is anyone else in your party able to access the
      bag?"* — ask about what she can know from where she stands, not the
      agent's own guess at the cause in TLC's words.
13. **[P, 20 October] Expand and Contract mean different things at the
    record and at her.**
    - **At the record (CRM searches):** Expand goes outward and down from
      what you have; Contract goes up to the parent — a name, contracted,
      finds the account it belongs to.
    - **With her:** Expand widens the line of inquiry — moving from her name
      and account to the issue at hand is an expansion. Contract focuses and
      continues the line of inquiry already open.
    - Runs 1–19 used my own meaning (Expand an open question, Contract a
      narrowing one); their labels are to be re-checked against this.
14. **[P]** Random: the player does not choose an angle. (§2)

## Targets — what a question can be aimed at

15. **[P]** A single word, already introduced by her or by the record.
    (§2; tentative, *Her side, mirrored*)
16. **[P, 19–20 October]** Words common to every customer are available from
    the start: *name*, *account*, *number*, *email*. Identifying her is
    suits played on them — Who·Name, What·Account, What·Number, Who·Email.
17. **[P, 19 October]** TLC's own internal terms — *permissions*,
    *Assign*, *Admin Only* — are known to the agent from the start.
18. **[P]** One or two inquiries a turn, each labelled on its own. (§2)

## Every play has a target, and most have a suit

19. **[P]** The three target classes: her, the record, a coworker — and the
    target decides the result. (§2, *Targets*) So searching and reading the
    record are suits played at the record, and the aside is a suit played
    at a coworker. Identifying her is suits played on the common words
    (rule 16). *(Phil, 20 October, correcting my draft, which said these
    carried no suit.)*
20. **[P, 20 October]** Offering a fix, next steps and promises are suits
    played on words of their own: What·Fix, What·Solution, Where·Fix,
    Who·Solution, Why·Fix, and so on; Who·Callback, When·Callback. *Fix*,
    *solution* and *callback* are among the words available to the agent.
21. **[P, 20 October]** The goodbye is probably the only move without a
    suit. It closes the call the way the universal opening starts it — where
    the customer shares her reason for contact — and like the opening it is
    free.

## What a search returns

22. **[P, 14 November]** The four landings of a move (misfire, flat, lands,
    critical) do not fit system searches well. A search comes back as one
    of six:
    - no results
    - one result
    - multiple results
    - many results
    - overwhelming results
    - exact match

    This agrees with the record: *"Inquire with the CRM, the CRM
    responds"*, and delivery is inert for the CRM (`settled.md` §2,
    *Targets*). What decides which of the six comes back is not yet ruled
    on.

## Telling the kind from the particular

23. **[P, 14 November]** *"It's about knowing the difference between general
    and generic, art and a work of art, or fact and opinion."* A name is a
    kind of thing many can have; the caller is one particular person. So a
    name search does not return one Sella Brandt, and "one result" is not
    the same as "exact match".
24. **[P, 14 November]** *"No one can tell them apart every time, that's
    the thing."* Not the new apprentice, and not anyone else. **[C]** So it
    is a draw for every agent: experience makes getting it right more
    reliable and never certain, which is §9's *reliability, never
    permission*.
25. **[P, 14 November]** A new apprentice should know to check a search
    result with her before opening it. **[C]** The check is a play.
26. **[C·ok, 14 November]** For the bag call, a search on her name alone
    returns multiple results: two Sella Brandts, a Stella Brandt and a
    Sela Brant, with the wrong Sella Brandt first. Her name with her party
    returns one result. The number on the bag returns an exact match.
27. **[C·ok, 14 November]** A new apprentice reads what came back
    correctly 65 times in 100. A guess, accepted to play with.

## The shape of a call — Phil, 19 November

28. **[P]** The opening is only ever: *"Thank you for contacting TLC, this
    is [Name]. How may I serve you?"* ("Serve", not "help": more medieval
    and fantasy-adjacent.)
29. **[P]** What comes between the opening and the case is the free
    middle: fluid, and dependent on how the interaction between customer
    and player plays out. That is what is being worked on.
30. **[P]** Every call ends with the agent creating the case, whatever the
    outcome. The case is the Mad Libs mechanic: the player builds the case
    notes from what the customer provided and what the player remembers.
    (The record already has the Mad Libs of the case, `settled.md`,
    *Coworker conversations*.)
31. **[P]** The case is a separate mechanic. The nine plays are for the
    interaction with the customer only.

## The ends of a call, and what lies between — Phil, 21 November

32. **[P]** The ends are known: the opening (greeting and inquiry) and the
    status decision (resolve, transfer or hold). What happens between is
    entirely dependent on what the customer provides.
33. **[P]** Creating the case and its status are interrelated (with rule
    30).
34. **[P]** Identifying information gives a finite set of outcomes: the
    match is found, it is not found, potential results that are valid, or
    potential results that are invalid. That set decides what the player
    can choose next; how the player proceeds drives the customer's choices
    for what happens next. Rule 22 is a similar version that splits the
    potential results into levels, without the yes/no. *(I had read the two
    as separate measures; that was wrong.)*
35. **[P, "I think"]** The moves available, and the approach to taking
    them, are where the suits and enhancements for the cards should be
    drawn from. Not ruled on: whether this replaces the six suits and four
    angles or decides which of them can be played.

## The fence, and where requests come from — Phil, 22 November

36. **[P, tentative: "I think so"]** An interaction is the convergence of
    an account, an individual and a request, and a fourth thing: the case,
    ticket or identifier, which is the end goal of every interaction and
    says what the request was about. Picture a triangular fenced area: the
    posts are the Account, the Individual and the Record of the
    Interaction. Inside the fence is the story, history, mythology, facts
    and opinions about the request, from those who were involved.
    **[C]** The suits and angles cover moving inside the fence; the moves
    with no card (`docs/moves-vs-suits.md`) belong to the posts: finding
    the account and individual (refining a search), what is put into the
    story (telling her, how a fix is said), and setting the third post (the
    status decision). The record's five categories of identifier (Account ·
    Contact · Case · Related Numbers · Related Details, `settled.md` §18)
    already name the three posts.
37. **[P]** Contemplate what types of request would likely come in for
    support. The universal greeting prompts the customer: it generates
    their account of the experience that led them to contact support. Their
    character sheet supplies the variables that make that explanation
    unique, and that explanation is what the player responds to on their
    first play. Finding what multiple customers have in common, and the
    play-throughs that follow, will show where descriptions overlap between
    categories of request.

## Cases, and what brings a customer to call — Phil, 24 November

38. **[P]** The result of every interaction is a case created or updated.
    To create a case you need the account, the individual and the request.
    To update one you need any of the case number, the individual or the
    account. A customer needs one thing to contact support: uncertainty.
39. **[P]** An update ultimately needs the request too, but without the
    existing case the request doesn't matter.
40. **[P]** Deciding whether to create or update is part of the agent's
    skill, and so is avoiding duplicate requests.
41. **[P]** When the account or individual can't be established, there is
    a generic placeholder, because wrong numbers happen and people with no
    current relationship may call.
42. **[P]** A customer will call about nearly anything: uncertainty about
    ANYTHING.
43. **[P]** Uncertainty comes from their experience, and is for the
    customer to explain.
44. **[P, "something like"]** How TLC would quantify requests, as
    categories on the case: Shipment · Order · Tracking · Navigation ·
    Access · Account · User · Repair. The customer describes an
    experience; TLC files it under a category.
45. **[P]** Shipment is about the goods themselves (wrong, damaged, short);
    Tracking is about where they are. Navigation is the customer not
    finding their way around TLC's services. Access, Account and User
    overlap: *"Welcome to my work life, categories that overlap and probably
    fit your description, however, someone else may think otherwise and
    choose differently."* **[C]** So the category on a case is a judgment
    call that differs from agent to agent, which is the record's point that
    records are a second imperfect memory (`settled.md` §8, *Emphasis,
    exceptions and records*, 3), and one way a later agent misses an
    existing case and duplicates it (rule 40).

## Handing a customer a request — Phil, 24 November

46. **[P]** A request need not be specific. A customer is handed a category
    and nothing else; what the character does in their life turns it into
    their own experience, which they then explain their own way. (Tracking:
    Hob's blade not back from repair; Wren's ingredients not arrived; Ilse's
    stock late; something for Sella's party that never reached them.)
47. **[C·ok, 24 November: "Record that"]** Handed a category they wouldn't
    normally deal with, a customer is out of their depth, and that makes a
    different call, not a broken one: they know less, so their uncertainty
    is bigger and they explain it worse, reaching for their own words where
    TLC has a proper term; they may not be the right person (Wren handed
    Account is calling about the guild's account, which isn't hers to change:
    she is ringing for her master, or isn't authorised, which brings the
    account and individual posts into play, and may end in a transfer or a
    case against the guild with her named as caller); their sheet decides
    how they handle it; and where it barely fits their life at all, it is
    the wrong number or the caller with no current relationship, and the
    generic placeholder (rule 41). Mismatches are not avoided: they are how
    the harder, stranger calls arise without anyone writing them.

## What customer baggage is — Phil, 24 November

48. **[P]** *"Customer baggage refers to a customer's past experiences,
    perceptions, and prior facts before an interaction that shape their
    expectations and how they view the service experience from any company
    or support staff or product or service they have dealt with or used
    previously."* His example: someone who doesn't like Ford vehicles
    because, from their experience, Ford means "Fix Or Repair Daily".
    *"Pell getting hurt isn't customer baggage at all. It is a worry, fear,
    concern, etc."*
    **[C]** By that definition, of the three accepted for the bag call on
    18 October, *no one told her* is baggage, *Pell gets hurt* is a worry,
    and *set it up herself* is a feeling about herself, not a view of the
    service. Hob's (told before he was mistaken), Wren's (passed between
    three people) and Ilse's (a callback that never came) are baggage.
49. **[P, 24 November]** Customer baggage is handed to a character the same
    way as a request: a broad kind, which their sheet interprets (what
    happened to them, how they feel about it, how it shows). Sella's *no one
    told her* is, broadly, the curse of knowledge: the assumption that no
    learning curve exists for the product, service or experience. The main
    categories, from CEB/Gartner's research and common service frameworks
    (Phil's sources: crmbuyer.com, *Reality check: your customers have
    baggage too*; destinationcrm.com, *Customers come with baggage*;
    twice.com, *Customer service rep as baggage handler, CEB*):
    - **Past interactions with your company:** unresolved issues, repeat
      contacts about the same problem, prior friction with frontline staff.
    - **Experiences with other companies:** habits, expectations or
      frustrations formed by dealing with competitors or unrelated
      businesses.
    - **Perceptions of service capabilities:** what the customer believes the
      team can or cannot do, including how much effort or enjoyment they
      expect from contacting support.
    - **Perceived value and relationship history:** how much value they feel
      they get, and how valued they feel as a customer.
    - **Pre-interaction information:** what the customer already knows, or
      thinks they know, going in, including inconsistent answers they
      received earlier.
    A first list of mine (left to work it out alone, not remembered,
    disbelieved, passed around, promise not kept, unreliable product,
    charged more than expected, kept waiting, blamed, treated well) was
    rough and is superseded by these.
50. **[P, 24 November: "2"]** Every customer has a position on all five
    kinds of baggage, like the other fields on their sheet. The request and
    the call decide which of them comes up.
51. **[C·ok, 24 November: "Sure let's test them with those"]** The five
    kinds of baggage are placed only by where the customer stands, never by
    what they do about it; how they feel and how it shows comes from the rest
    of their sheet. Past interactions with TLC: poor · mixed or none · good.
    Experiences with other companies: poor · mixed or none · good. What they
    think support can do: little · some · a lot. How valued they feel: not
    much · somewhat · very. What they know going in: little · some · a lot.
    (A first version wrote what each band made a customer do, which would
    have given every character the same reactions.)
