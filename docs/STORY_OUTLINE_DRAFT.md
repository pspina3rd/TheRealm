# Mana Crystals — second-half outline draft

**Status: writer proposal for review, not approved canon or implemented content.**
The order and rough pacing come from [STORY_ARC.md](STORY_ARC.md). Every new
region name, boss identity, motive, and supernatural mechanism below
is proposed. Fennick's role and the Master's identity are not established
by that brief; this draft offers one coherent interpretation of each without
claiming either was present in the existing script.

Character development precedes dialogue. The working profiles in
[CHARACTERS_DRAFT.md](CHARACTERS_DRAFT.md) distinguish existing script evidence
from proposed characterization. Earlier dialogue samples have been withdrawn;
new scene dialogue is collected in [DIALOGUE_DRAFT.md](DIALOGUE_DRAFT.md),
drafted from the characters and their immediate stakes.
The [scene treatment](SCENES_DRAFT.md) develops this outline into specific
wants, conflicts, choices, and emotional changes that guide the dialogue.

Continuity source: the [team's updated story script](https://github.com/pspina3rd/TheRealm/blob/fe5c3e49473297c008b2167f8476c043db735d0f/docs/STORY.md).
Its opening, Refusal, mentor scenes, Wyrm's survival, and chapter hook take
precedence over this draft's earlier assumptions. They are consulted here;

The [team's encounter appendix](https://github.com/pspina3rd/TheRealm/blob/d14b3c4f6bb173986e05af4ce89e9895c437a34b/docs/ENCOUNTER_DESIGN.md)

## The dramatic spine

The hero begins by fixing things people can touch: water, soil, lamps, roads.
At the midpoint, defeating the Moon Wyrm appears to fix the cause. The
Master's voice escaping its body proves that a victory over one creature
cannot repair the whole injury. The second half asks the hero to understand
what they are breaking before striking again, while continuing to help
people with immediate needs.

Proposed interpretation: the Master is an older, disembodied will that used
the Wyrm as its mouth. The updated script establishes an obedient vessel,
but not the voice's age or the animal's degree of agency. Vesper's
belief in the Wyrm and the voice was sincere, but incomplete. Their later
choices remain theirs, including the final choice to accept the voice.

## Continuity before the descent

Keep the team's existing first-half dialogue. Its updated opening already
limits the sleeping households; Rowan, Bean (formerly Bea), Ordor (Odo),
Pip, and Pip's mother remain awake. The Refusal supplies the personal push:
Rowan learns that his son Hareguy has slept three weeks at the Brightwater
inn, and gives the hero his old stone because he cannot make the road himself.
The blight rising from below makes lasting repairs another reason to continue.

This chooses the permitted alternative to making Pip's mother the personal
inciting loss. His pudding, cabbage, kitchen, and impending-punishment lines
continue to make literal sense. Do not quietly reinterpret all his present
tense speech as memories or add a sleeping-relative subplot to compensate.

## 50% — the Moon Wyrm: Ordeal and false victory

Preserve the team's aftermath: the animal survives and sleeps, moonlight
returns, the violet stone glows, and Brightwater sleepers stir. At Sevenstones,
the voice speaks without the Wyrm, an empty cloak and wet footprints mark
a passage toward the ward, and an old man recognizes their maker. The
false victory preserves those real gains while revealing a threat that survived.

Proposed continuation: investigate the ward with the old man's help and
restore a controlled passage into the Sunken Archive. Do not invent a fissure
that bypasses the ward scene. The inner containment chamber remains intact
for the ending. The current next-chapter seal is an implementation boundary;
its opening needs a future playable transition, not an unexplained new key.

## 50–56% — proposed region: the Sunken Archive

**Goal:** find a usable descent and learn why the ward survived the Wyrm.
Flooded storage galleries, broken lifts, and a small refuge provide a full
region of exploration, ordinary fights, trade, and repair puzzles. The
refuge's proposed waterkeeper, Odile, needs working drains before sharing the lower route;
the player earns cooperation through the same kinds of practical service
that grounded Mossvale.

Records describe repeated attempts to give the voice a body. Crucially,
those attempts predate the Wyrm. The reversal is that the ward was built
to contain something below, not merely keep travelers away. The player
restores a controlled passage rather than smashing it open. A watercourse
through the restored drains leads to the next inhabited basin.

## 56–62% — proposed region: the Cistern Marches; Boss 2

**Goal:** restore water to the refuge and reach the deeper road. Several
reservoir settlements trade access, medicines, and engineering knowledge.
Their disputes make the region social as well as hostile; an early shortcut
is optional, while a safe supply route rewards exploration.

**Boss 2: Irdrath**, the team's ash-and-smoke figure around a buried
Firstcoal. Proposed regional role: his heat tends the cistern works and
sleeping chambers. He knows their temperatures and pressure changes, but
rations water and hardens ash across the routes rather than trusting others
to move the residents beyond his care. This is proposed material agency,
not a renamed human official with an invented past.

At roughly 62%, Irdrath attacks when Odile's carriers begin using a safely
tested independent drain. The ash, smoke, and Firstcoal confrontation leaves
the chamber walls intact; exact phases remain encounter-design work. Freeing
the coal disrupts his control without removing it as loot. After defeat,
diminished smoke clears the arch while Irdrath still worries about cold
feet and the residents awaiting transfer. The first stretcher goes through;
the hero gains a maintenance route rather than a boss key.

## 62–67% — proposed region: the Rootways

**Goal:** follow that route without abandoning the people dependent on it.
Huge roots cross an abandoned transport network. Shelter repairs, herb
trades, and route-finding separate Boss 2 from Vesper. The roots connect
surface blight to the deep system, giving the player visible consequences
of helping underground settlements rather than a sequence of locked doors.

Vesper is encountered at a distance and then in conversation. They insist
the voice's departure proves the Master's strength is not confined to one body, but their
certainty slips when the hero cites the older records. The hero can learn
why Vesper surrendered to that promise without earning an automatic
redemption through attitude points. A repaired tram reaches the Glass Warrens.

## 67–72% — proposed region: the Glass Warrens; Vesper duel

**Goal:** prevent Vesper from opening a faster route for the voice. Reflective
crystal chambers require orientation puzzles and encounters with people
whose voices are being repeated through the stone. Ketter, the team's
lampkeeper in the proposed role of bellwright, is stranded by a damaged
landing. The player follows their moving lamp rather than empty echoes.
Their rescue introduces the different marks for the voice branch and lamp
feed; preserving the carriers' lights matters in the coming duel.

Vesper wants their pursuit of power and knowledge vindicated. The older
records challenge their claim to understand what they serve; curiosity
competes with pride. Fear of being replaced can add pressure, but does not
replace their established ambition with a simple wish for approval.
At roughly 72%, they choose to keep control of the route by force.
This is Vesper in their own body and skills, not the final transformation.

Defeat costs Vesper control of the route. They retreat alive, refusing the
hero's offer to stop. The player closes the upper voice branch while
preserving Ketter's lamp feed; the central relay farther below remains active. Vesper's later return follows a choice, not an unexplained
resurrection.

## 72–77% — proposed region: the Bell Hollows

**Goal:** find how the remaining passage works before closing it. Old bells
carry signals through the deep chambers. Artisans maintain them because
they guide travelers and support their settlements; destroying them all
would repeat Irdrath's mistake. The player repairs safe relays, rescues
stranded workers, and separates a useful network from the voice abusing it.

Develop the ward's old man through correspondence about an unfinished rite.
Proposed identification: he is Fennick, and the boy he taught to walk is
Vesper. Neither name is confirmed by the existing hook. This gives his work
a personal stake without inventing a new encounter to introduce him late.
Proposed role: he knows the rite's communal portion, but lacks deep evidence
needed to finish it. His relationship to the hero begins at the ward.
At Ketter's workshop, a repaired local circuit lets them hand a shift to a
relief operator. Their plans and Fennick's letters reveal the surface
stations and a missing lower return. An inspection-rail diagram completes
the gap before the relay closes; the hero copies it into the letter.

## 77–82% — proposed region: the Quiet Foundry; Boss 3

**Goal:** shut down the central relay while preserving the settlements'
essential routes. Repair work, machinery puzzles, and ordinary encounters
make a substantial region before the third named boss.

**Boss 3: the Lishal**, the team's seven singers, each associated with a
colour and column. Proposed regional role: they coordinate a sounding frame
and offer permanent sleep as freedom from grief and need. Their food,
bandages, and shelter provide real care, but when a worker refuses the
sleep knowingly, individual singers close doors and block the exit. A
singer objects, then rejoins the frame; another turns from an injured worker
back to the crystals. Responsibility remains individual as well as collective.

Before the relay closes, Vesper accepts the voice in full knowledge of the
earlier vessels. The hero opens a safe footbridge for retreat, then must
hold a shutter brake to protect escaping workers. Vesper consciously refuses
the exit and leaves by the service ascent. At roughly 82%, all seven Lishal
choose a manual override after the hero disconnects the coercive branch.
Defeating them preserves the local lift signals and stops the broadcast;
it cannot remove what Vesper already accepted. The rite's missing return
has been copied before this fight, not supplied as arbitrary boss loot.
No separate Master fight is added.

## 82–85% — proposed region: the Upward Road

**Goal:** bring the procedure to Sevenstones. The repaired underground routes
now help people return; the player sees earlier aid repaid in shelter,
directions, and transport. A collapsed ascent requires one final group
repair, giving the return journey playable substance without another boss.

Vesper's changed voice reaches the hero once. Its language still carries
Vesper's wounded pride and determination, now made threatening by the power they sought.
The hero returns with a way to act and people who have reasons to help.

## 85–95% — Sevenstones and Fennick's rite

Fennick compares the recovered procedure with the surface practice. Proposed
function: the rite prevents the voice from escaping into another vessel
when Vesper falls. It does not kill Vesper remotely or undo the final fight.
The copied procedure depicts the wickless brass lantern at the outer ring.
A pale-light test checks station connections; it does not prove that a
living voice can be held. That expectation comes from the recovered records
and is confirmed only when Vesper's presence meets the active boundary.

Spread preparation across return visits and local tasks: reopen a transport
route, obtain a volunteered ritual material, and protect the gathered people.
Draw on relationships the hero actually formed, with fallback contributions
for optional quests. Do not require every mentor spell or a particular class.
Mossvale's awake residents still have ordinary responsibilities and voices.

The rite is the road back made communal: the hero accepts help rather than
returning as a solitary cure. Its successful conclusion contains the threat
long enough for the final approach. It does not restore every sleeper yet.

## 95–100% — the Malificat, Vesper's final form, and return with the elixir

The final approach uses a prepared, contained space near Sevenstones,
provisionally the ward's inner chamber. Vesper now embodies the power they
chose, while recognizable gestures and language preserve continuity with
the earlier duel. Their grievance is personal even as the voice tries to
speak through them.

Retain the team's tentative Resurrection and lantern beat late in this final
battle. This draft proposes Fennick as the speaker of the source phrase
"Ves, en." without inventing a translation. Lantern light makes room for the
hero's remaining effort; it does not win the battle. The appendix's phase,
preview-fog, and six-row effects remain encounter-design proposals, with
chains and desaturation visual only, normal controls, and a visible current
landing and skyline. Its final element has a mentor-or-alignment fallback.

The final fight is the Resurrection test of everything learned: strength
can matter because the player first protected others, understood the
mechanism, and accepted help. Defeating this form ends the voice's escape
cycle. The scene treatment selects survival without borrowed power as a
proposed ending to evaluate; Vesper's fate is not approved canon.
The immediate outcome is definite within this proposal: the voice is
contained and the unnatural sleep begins to lift.

Return with the elixir is playable aftermath or an epilogue grounded in
particular households waking, water running, and repaired roads carrying
people home. Losses are not erased wholesale. Full-campaign completion
belongs here. The Wyrm remains the essential midpoint victory that taught
the hero why the journey had to continue.
