Follows `docs/baggage-24-nov.md`. Under good baggage, Hob, Wren and Ilse
converged on *"I have the order number"*. Phil, 24 November: test solutions

(*"Use those two mixes, run both tests"*).

- **Test 2:** the writer works out what *knows a lot* means for each person
  from their sheet and notebook, before writing (Profile B, 8 openings).
  `logs/baggage-24-nov/test2-knows-a-lot.md`
- **Test 3:** Mix C (TLC poor · others good · some · somewhat · knows a lot)
  and Mix D (TLC good · others poor · a lot · not much · knows little),
  written the old way (16 openings). `logs/baggage-24-nov/test3-mixes.md`

Both used Sonnet, one run each. The experiences were fixed, word for word
from the first test. One change of method from the first test **[C]**: its
rule to rewrite customers until they read differently was removed, because
it would hide the convergence being measured.

## What they show

**Test 2: interpreting *a lot* did not separate them.** The writer gave
each person their own reasoning, but Hob, Wren and Ilse landed on the same
content: what was ordered, when it was due, and the order number. All three
openings say they have the order number, as in the first test. The same
step also made all four open by giving their name.

**Test 3, Mix C: the same fact, but different people.** All four mention
the order number or account details. The rest of the opening is each
person's own:
- Hob insists the details are right.
- Wren says she checked twice.
- Ilse asks what happens next.
- Sella says the last call took too long.

**Test 3, Mix D: a new shared line.** A good history with TLC came out as
*"you've helped before"* for Hob, Ilse and Sella (twice). *Knows little*
came out as *"I don't know why"* in three of the four Access openings.

**Across all three tests:** each position gets said out loud as a line to
the agent. The sheet changes how the line is said, but the position
decides what it is. Every opening carries all five kinds at once, which
crowds out the person.
