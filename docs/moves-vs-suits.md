# The moves the bag call offers, against the suits and angles — 21 November

Phil, 21 November: the moves available, and the approach to taking them,
may be where the suits and enhancements should be drawn from; it could be a
new way to look at them, a different description, a way to improve them, a
red herring, or something else. This table is the test (agreed: *"Yes, do
the table"*).

**How it was made [C, mine]:** for each moment in the bag call, the moves
an agent actually has there, taken from the calls played on 14–20 November
and from the record (searches, the profile, Bram, fixes, the status
decision). Each move is mapped to a suit and angle by `docs/card-rules.md`:
the suit is the kind of answer asked for (rule 8); Validate checks with
her, by a guess or a paraphrase (11); Verify checks against a source, or
asks her to go and look (12); with her, Expand starts another line and
Contract stays on the open one; at the record, Expand goes outward and
down and Contract goes up to the parent (13); fixes, solutions and next
steps are suits on their own words (20).

**Fit:** *clean* (one suit and angle, no stretching) · *loose* (it can be
labelled, but the label does not say what the move is) · *none*.

## What the table shows

**Most moves fit.** Every question to her, every check with her, every
look at the record that goes from one thing to the next, the coworker, and
the fix and next steps, map cleanly onto a suit and angle.

**Four kinds of move do not:**
1. **Refining a search.** After too many results, no results, or the wrong
   spelling, searching again on a narrower or different term comes up
   after three of the six outcomes in section 3, and neither angle at the
   record describes it.
2. **Statements that are not questions.** An apology or an acknowledgement
   before asking anything, and telling her something new (what the profile
   says). Rule 10 makes a statement an inquiry because it invites a yes or
   no; these do not, or do something else first.
3. **How a fix is said.** The suit names the remedy; nothing carries the
   explanation, or the difference between saying it without blame and
   telling her she did it, which is the difference that decided three of
   the ten calls on 14 November.
4. **The status decision.** Resolve and hold have no card; transfer has one
   only when she asks for someone else.

**Every suit and every angle was used somewhere**, Random included (as a
fumble). None is surplus.

**Which of Phil's five it looks like, my reading [C]:** not a red herring,
and not a replacement. The suits and angles cover what happens *between*
two people asking and answering. Where they fall short is where the move
is not a question: refining a search, telling, how a remedy is put, and
deciding the status. Drawing cards from the moves the moment offers would
add those, and would also say which existing cards make sense when. So it
looks closest to **a method to improve** the system: the moment decides
which cards can be played, and supplies the few that the suits do not
cover.
