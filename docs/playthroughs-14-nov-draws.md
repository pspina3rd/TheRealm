Same call and same customer as `docs/playthroughs-14-nov.md` (Sheet Y; her
own setting, changed last night; profile reads *Party bag access: Admin
Only — changed last night*). Two things are new, both agreed with Phil on

  volume, signal, certainty, accuracy, openness
  (`docs/claims-her-side.md`, 16–24).
- **The agent is a general new apprentice** with a sheet of their own, so
  their moves can land badly.

The draws come from a seeded script, `logs/draws-14-nov.js`, so that I am
not choosing where either of them lands. Its output is in
`logs/draws-14-nov.txt`. **Every number in the script is my guess.** The
lines are mine, made up in play to fit the draws, and are not canon.

## The apprentice's sheet [C, accepted by Phil to try]

- **Focus:** procedure first. Of Phil's questions they mostly reach for
  *what didn't I understand?* and *what's most urgent?*, which to them means
  their own next step: name, search, profile, fix, next steps.
- **Strengths:** every card; the record and coworkers; no assumptions.
- **Weaknesses:** cards misfire or land flat; they go back to the same
  door; they search too soon; when unsure of the angle they say something
  anyway (Random); traits not yet grown.
- **Knows:** the opening, the six questions, how to search, TLC's terms by
  name.
- **Doesn't know:** her words for our terms (*transfer* is not *Assign* to
  them); what is going on at TLC; how a setting looks from her side.

## How to read a play

- **Reaches for** is the one question of Phil's the apprentice went to,
  drawn.
- **Landing** is how their move came off, drawn: *misfire*, *flat*,
  *lands* or *critical* (the record's four, `settled.md` §8 *The engine*).
- **Her draws** list only what was off her usual. *Low* and *high* are
  ordinary variation; *very low* and *very high* are her tails.
- *Goes back to the same door* means the same angle as their last question
  to her (`settled.md` §3).

> **AGENT** Thank you for contacting TLC, this is [AgentName], how may I help you?

## What happened

**Outcomes:** three of five fixed (runs 1, 3, 4), none of them cleanly.
Two ran out of plays (runs 2, 5). With the perfect agent of the earlier
file, all five were fixed.

**She stayed herself.** Her facts never moved, and she never said anything
outside Phil's list. Her worry about Pell came out in four runs, each time
when openness drew high, and each time meaning the same thing. Being asked
the same thing twice wore thin both times it happened (runs 2 and 5).

**Her answers varied in size and clearness, not in content.** A good
question sometimes got three words (run 2, play 4) and a botched one
sometimes got everything (run 2, play 5; run 5, play 3). That is her
curves doing the work, not the agent's skill.

**Her loose wording misled the apprentice once** (run 1: "out on a
contract" sounded as if Pell had already gone). One question cleared it.

**She was sure and wrong once, and it helped** (run 1, play 6: "Nothing
changed. I was in there half the night"). The wrong part did no harm, and
the rest told the apprentice what had happened.

**The apprentice never connected *transfer* to *Assign* without help.**
In run 4 it took a guess checked with her and then Bram. In no run was
she shown how to Assign.

## What is wrong

1. **The apprentice's focus is drawn fresh every play, so it ignores how
   many plays are left.** Runs 2 and 5 failed because the draw kept
   saying "what didn't I understand?" on plays 8 and 9 with nothing looked
   up. That may be a true picture of a new apprentice, or it may be my
   rule being too mechanical.
2. **Most of her answers have at least one count off her usual,** because
   there are five of them: only 2 of 50 were all usual. A tail came up in
   18 of the 50, which is more often than "rare".
3. **What flat, lands and critical mean at the record is mine.** I played
   flat as "finds it and doesn't connect it" and critical as "finds more
   than was searched for". The record gives the four landings but not what
   they mean for a lookup.
