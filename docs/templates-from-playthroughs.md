# Templates from the play-throughs — 10 October

Working material, not the record. Nothing here is settled. The authority is
`docs/settled.md` §8, *Remembrances and lenses*: the templates are the Mad
Libs a customer applies her word cards to, developed from delegated
play-throughs, and what is sought is **the customers' patterns**.

## What was played

Five callers, each through the same four calls twice (an ordinary morning,
and already worn down): 40 play-throughs, 360 replies, 100 asides. The files
are in `logs/playthroughs-10-oct/`, with the brief the agents were given.

- **Sella**, with the remembrances from the record.
- Four **test callers** with known manners, borrowed from Oz: Dorothy, the
  Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, the Lion. Each was given a life by answering
  the six questions. They are for finding patterns and are not people in the
  record.

Played by a mid-sized model, one agent per caller. Their reports were not
taken on trust: the files were checked for existence, exact agent lines, no
sentence reused by a caller (0 of 550), the word *transfer* never volunteered
(0), and no feeling named as a label by the mechanical test (0).

**A model played these, not real callers.** The patterns below are how a
model thinks callers talk. They are candidates until Phil has read them.

## The templates

A blank is in `[CAPITALS]`. "Seen in" counts how many of the five callers
used the shape; a shape one caller used is that caller's habit, not a
pattern, and is not listed.

### 1. They'd have had this
`[PERSON] would've [KNOWN / HAD / SORTED] [THIS], [TAG].`
- Fills from: a companion, someone lost, a rival.
- Seen in 4 of 5.
- *"Dessa would know the exact count if you need it to the coin."* ·
  *"Ode would've had the exact count."* · *"We used to have a healer who'd
  have sorted this in her sleep."* · approved earlier: *"Les would've known
  what to do."*

### 2. Feels like
`[THIS] feels like [A REMEMBRANCE, AS ONE IMAGE] — [WHAT THE TWO SHARE].`
- Fills from: what annoys her, what she does for fun, an enemy, an adventure.
- Seen in 4 of 5.
- *"Feels like the guild clerk, honestly — fill in one form, then another to
  say you filled in the first."* · *"Feels like watching Gregor Roman wrestle
  — you know how it ends, but you keep hoping."* · *"like arguing fare with
  that ferryman at the ford, he never budges either."* · *"feels a bit like
  the time a broker smiled at us and still paid half the job."*
- This is the shape that lets something from her life land in the middle of
  a call about something else.

### 3. Before anyone noticed
`[COMPANION] [DID THE HARD THING] [WHEN] before any of us [NOTICED], [AND I'D RATHER THIS DIDN'T GO THE SAME WAY].`
- Fills from: a companion. It is a trait shown through something that
  happened.
- Seen in 4 of 5.
- *"Marl walked two days on a turned ankle last month before any of us
  noticed."* · *"Pell's carried half of that on his back before now and
  never once said it was heavy."* · *"Fenn carries half of it on his back
  without complaint."*

### 4. Said it already
`I [FEEL LIKE I'VE / KEEP] [SAID THAT / SAYING THE SAME THINGS] [HOW OFTEN][, SORRY].`
- Fills from: nothing in her life. It is what she does when the agent asks
  again.
- Seen in 4 of 5.
- *"I keep saying the same things, sorry."* · *"I don't love saying the same
  list twice in one morning, but there it is."* · *"I've said that part a few
  times now, I think."*

### 5. Once, so now
`We [LOST / NEARLY LOST] [WHAT] [WHEN], [SO / AND] I [WHAT I DO NOW].`
- Fills from: a tragedy, an adventure.
- Seen in 3 of 5.
- *"We lost our mule and half our gear in a river crossing last spring, so I
  watch that bag closer than I used to."* · *"Coe nearly traded his boots for
  a lantern at a market once, and I've kept a closer eye on what goes in and
  out of that bag since."*

### 6. No sleep
`[DIDN'T SLEEP MUCH / BARELY SLEPT / UP HALF THE NIGHT], [TAG].`
- A feeling, never named as one.
- Seen in 3 of 5.
- *"Didn't sleep much, if I'm honest."* · *"Barely slept after, kept thinking
  I'd missed a step somewhere."* · *"two last night when I couldn't sleep for
  thinking about it."*

### 7. Who that is
`[NAME], [WHO THEY ARE TO US], [ONE THING THAT HAPPENED].`
- How a person gets introduced in passing.
- Seen in 3 of 5.
- *"For Coe — he took a bad knock three towns back and hasn't quite shaken it
  off."* · *"He's the newest of us, joined after we lost our last healer."* ·
  *"what doesn't fit goes on Biscuit, our mule."*

### 8. What they keep saying
`[COMPANION] [KEEPS SAYING / THINKS / HAS STOPPED ASKING] [WHAT], [WHICH ISN'T HELPING / WHICH MIGHT BE TRUE].`
- Fills from: a companion.
- Seen in 2 of 5.
- *"Rennik keeps saying it's never let him down before, which isn't
  helping."* · *"Wren thinks I'm making it harder than it needs to be, which,
  honestly, might be true."* · *"Perrin's stopped even asking if it worked
  yet."*

### 9. What he'd say about me
`There's [A MAN] at [PLACE], [ENEMY], who'd say [A JUDGEMENT OF ME]; today [HE'D HAVE A POINT].`
- Fills from: an enemy. The short form is `[ENEMY] would love this.`
- Seen in 2 of 5.
- *"There's a fellow at the guild hall, Ostlin, who'd probably say I can't
  find my own feet most mornings — today he might have a point."* · *"Dreff
  would love this, honestly, he already thinks we're useless."*

### 10. With all this going on
`I nearly [FORGOT / MISSED] [A THING FROM MY LIFE] [THIS MONTH], with all this going on.`
- Fills from: a tragedy, or what she does for fun. It lets a heavy thing out
  as an errand.
- Seen in 2 of 5.
- *"I nearly forgot to pay my brother's dues this month with all this going
  on."* · *"I spent last evening patching boots instead of dealing with
  this."*

## The word cards — what differed between callers

The shapes above were shared. These were not, and they are what made one
caller sound unlike another.

- **Sella** compares things to the clerk and his forms; "honestly"; "no
  less".
- **Dorothy** adds little; plain; "if that matters"; "but there it is".
- **The Scarecrow** says sorry, takes the blame, and lets his enemy's opinion
  of him stand: "which might be true", "today he'd have a point".
- **The Tin Woodman** tucks the aside into the middle of a sentence; "if I'm
  honest"; "I'd rather this not go the same way".
- **The Lion** reports what his people say and argues with it: "which isn't
  helping", "that's not the point, is it?"

## One memory, read several ways

What §8 says should happen did happen. The same remembrance came out
differently each time:

- Sella's brother: *"I nearly forgot to pay my brother's dues this month with
  all this going on."* · *"I'm so tired I nearly paid my brother's guild dues
  twice this month."* · *"my head's not right this morning, I nearly forgot my
  brother's dues too."* None says who he is or what happened to him.
- Sella's old partner: *"My old partner runs her own now, out of the same
  hall."* · *"My old partner would've sorted this in two minutes."*
- The Tin Woodman's Marl and the ankle: four readings across four calls.

## When and where callers added something

- An aside came in 100 of 360 replies. Starting worn down doubled it (68
  against 32).
- 77 came with an answer and 23 in place of one. Only 6 were in a caller's
  first reply.
- Most sat at the end of the reply. The Tin Woodman's sat in the middle.
- Asked two things at once, callers answered **both** 155 times out of 160.
  They took one and left the other 3 times.
- How often varied by caller: the Scarecrow 30, the Tin Woodman 28, Sella 17,
  Dorothy 14, the Lion 11.

## What is wrong with the play-throughs

Read as a caller would hear them. These are faults in the material, not in
the templates.

- **Some lines say what should be left unsaid:** *"doesn't help my mood this
  morning"*, *"which worries me more than it should"*, *"not that I miss
  working with her"*.
- **Some are too clever to be said aloud:** *"mocking me"*, *"this morning
  feels like it's earned a page."*
- **The callers made up how the account pages work:** *"I pick his name, hit
  go"*, *"find the word, pick him, send it."* The brief gave no such facts.
- **Sella's recipient is wrong in places.** The brief did not say the draught
  is for Pell, and the agent chose Tobin.
- **Nearly every aside was tied back to the call.** Phil said it does not
  have to relate to the call at all. Asides with no tie were rare: *"Ode
  still hasn't let me forget he owes me a bowstring."* · *"Rennik's the one
  who keeps the maps, swears he's never once been wrong about one."*

## After an independent read — which templates survive

Every one of the 360 replies was then read by an independent reader at the
same level as the author, against Phil's standard and his accepted and
rejected lines. I checked seven of its quotes against the file; all seven
were there as quoted.

**Its verdict:** not drivel throughout. About two replies in three are plain
answers a caller could say. About a third fail, and the failures sit in the
lines meant to carry character — which are the asides these templates were
drawn from. 108 of 360 replies carried a flag; 2 of 40 calls had none.

| What it flagged | Count |
|---|---|
| A fact about the problem the caller made up | 35 |
| Written, not spoken (a simile or a turned phrase) | 21 |
| Says what should be left unsaid | 21 |
| A trait read out | 19 |
| Sharp with nothing to cause it | 7 |
| Does not answer | 3 |
| Other | 2 |

By caller: the Scarecrow 29, Dorothy 22, Sella 20, the Lion 19, the Tin
Woodman 18. Worn-down calls carried 70 flags against 38.

**What that does to the ten templates above:**

- **2, Feels like — fails.** Almost every use was flagged as written, not
  spoken: the clerk's forms, Gregor Roman, the ferryman, the broker. It is a
  writer's simile.
- **9, What he'd say about me — fails.** Flagged as written every time. The
  Ostlin lines end on a quip.
- **5, Once, so now — the second half fails.** "So I watch that bag closer
  than I used to" explains what the first half already carried.
- **3, Before anyone noticed — only as something that happened.** "Marl
  walked two days on a turned ankle" was called the best thread in the file.
  "Carries half of it without complaint" and "never says when something's
  wrong" were flagged as traits read out.
- **10, With all this going on — only the plain form.** "I nearly forgot to
  pay my brother's dues this month" passed. "I'm so tired I nearly…" and "my
  head's not right this morning…" were flagged for saying the feeling.
- **1, They'd have had this — passes without a tail.** "…she keeps better
  track than I do" was flagged as a trait.
- **7, Who that is — passes.** Two of the ten best lines are this shape.
- **8, What they keep saying — passes.** One of the ten best.
- **6, No sleep — passes once.** It turned up in nearly every worn-down call
  and read as a mood setting.
- **4, Said it already — passes only when the agent really has asked
  before.** Seven uses came the first time something was asked.

**What the ten best lines have in common:** a named person, one thing that
happened, and then the caller stops. *"For Coe — he took a bad knock three
towns back and hasn't quite shaken it off."* · *"We used to have a healer
who'd have sorted this in her sleep, before she walked off after the
cave-in."* · *"Rafe near walked us off a washed-out bridge last spring
trusting his own map."* · *"Rennik keeps saying it's never let him down
before, which isn't helping."*

**Counts corrected.** A search of every reply puts *Said it already* and *No
sleep* in 5 of 5 callers and *Who that is* in 4 of 5, not the 4, 3 and 3 I
counted by eye above.

**Faults in my brief that caused flags:**

- It told worn-down callers this was "not their first attempt to get help".
  Eleven replies then claimed earlier calls, which the reader counted as
  made-up facts.
- It gave no facts about the account pages, and the agent's questions ask
  callers to talk through what they did. Sixteen replies invented taps,
  chimes and menus.
- It did not say who Sella's draught is for.
- It asked for non-sequiturs, and the callers reached for similes to tie
  them to the call.

**Stock shared between callers**, which would be mistaken for a pattern: a
map-reader who is never wrong appears in three parties; "keeps better track
than I do" is word for word in two callers; "hasn't moved an inch" is said by
all five; each caller has two or three anecdotes that come round again.

## Second run, with the brief corrected

Same five callers, same four calls, same two starting states: 40
play-throughs, 360 replies, in `logs/playthroughs-10-oct-second/`. The brief
was changed to stop the faults it had caused: no describing the account's
pages, no earlier calls, fuller lives so an anecdote need not come round
twice, no similes or quips or neat last lines, asides not tied back to the
call, and the lines Phil rejected added as examples of what not to do. The
callers were **not** given the five surviving shapes.

**Mechanical check.** No simile bridges, no semicolons, no earlier calls, one
interface word (was sixteen replies). 65 asides (was 100); 59 came with an
answer and 6 in place of one; none in a caller's first reply.

**Independent read, same reader standard.** I checked six of its quotes; all
were there.

|---|---|---|
| Replies flagged | 108 of 360 | 50 of 360 |
| Calls with no flag | 2 of 40 | 8 of 40 |
| Written, not spoken | 21 | 5 |
| Says the unsaid | 21 | 4 |

| Made-up fact about the problem | 35 | 22 |

| Does not answer | 3 | 6 |

| Other | 2 | 4 |

Its verdict: "not drivel line by line: roughly six in seven caller lines are
plain, sayable phone speech, and a dozen of the asides would pass for a real
person. It still reads as generated across the set, because the asides arrive
unbridged and on a quota, the same few templates recur under different names,
and one caller repeatedly invents what the account's pages did."

**What is still wrong:**

- **Made-up facts (22).** Mostly "I pick it, try to send it over to him", and
  the Tin Woodman "trying the closest thing". The agent's questions keep
  asking callers to talk through what they did, and the scenario gives them
  nothing true to say.
- **An aside in place of an answer reads as not listening (6).** "What have
  you tried?" — "I pitch horseshoes at the post behind the inn most
  evenings."
- **A death dropped after a routine question (4).** The wording passes; the
  moment does not. "It started last night?" — "Yes. My brother went down into
  the Dungeon with his party four years ago."
- **Asides arrive on a quota.** Each caller produced them at a steady rate
  whatever was happening.

### The shapes the reader found on its own

It was asked, separately, to describe what recurs among the asides that pass,
across different callers. It had not seen the ten above.

1. **A name, one thing that person did, stop.** 5 of 5. *"Fenwick walked
   twelve miles back once for a boot I'd left at a campsite."* · *"Ossie fell
   clean through a rotten floor at the old mill last month and came up
   laughing about it."* — This is shapes 3 and 7 above, found twice by two
   readers.
2. **Someone at the hall, or an official, who keeps doing a petty thing, in
   the present tense.** 4 of 5. *"The clerk at the hall wants a form for
   everything…"* · *"…still writes wanting more coin."*
3. **A small thing broken, and nobody has fixed it.** 3 of 5. *"The guild
   hall door's been sticking for a week and nobody's fixed that either."* ·
   *"Wheels on the wagon have been squealing since yesterday and nobody's
   greased them."*
4. **We lost some days once because someone was sure.** 3 of 5.
5. **A pastime the caller is bad at, with a flat second clause.** 3 of 5.
   *"I play cards with Bramwell most evenings, and I keep losing to the same
   bluff."*
6. **A toll warden and the rate.** 3 of 5 — the same content, not only the
   same shape.
7. **Something kept in my own pack, not the bag.** 2 of 5.
8. **Unsigned notes saying they are being watched.** 2 of 5.

Outside the passing set: **a loss, then the one object left** (the whetstone,
the hat, the pack) — good wording, flagged for where it was dropped.

### A flaw in both runs: the callers were not independent

Both briefs carried Sella's life in full, and Phil's accepted lines, and every
agent read the whole brief. The four test callers were told not to reuse
Sella's, but several of the "shared" shapes are things on Sella's sheet:

- the clerk and his forms → shape 2;
- the map held upside down → shape 4;
- whittling, and backing a wrestler who loses → shape 5;
- the notes about minions → shape 8.

So for those four, recurrence across callers may be Sella's sheet echoed, not
a pattern in how callers talk. The shapes that owe nothing to her sheet are
**1** (a name and one thing they did), **3** (a small thing broken),
**7** (my own pack), and from the first run *they'd have had this* and
*what they keep saying*.

### The shapes it found, with nothing of Sella to echo

It was asked which shapes recur across different callers among the asides
that pass, and whether each reads as a real habit of speech or as stock.

**Real habits**

1. **The remark hangs off the thing just named.** An item or a person comes up
   in the answer, and the next clause is a story about that item or person.
   3 of 4. *"Corvin sold Bren a blade once that snapped first swing, this
   one's held up better."* (after naming the blade) · *"Toby's the one waiting
   on this, he banged his leg up…"* — "This is the one thing that separates
   the passes from the flags."
2. **Something happened, and here is how things have been since.** 3 of 4.
   *"…and he's been slow since."* · *"…before she went lame outside Carrow
   Ford, now it's all on our backs."* It leaves the feeling out and lets the
   consequence carry it.
3. **Tied to last night.** What someone did, or what the caller missed, while
   she was up with the problem. 3 of 4. *"Sabra took first watch again last
   night so I could sit with this instead of sleeping."* · *"I usually just
   sit and soak my feet in the river of an evening. Didn't get the chance
   last night."*
4. **A companion's standing habit, marked by one small word: always, still,
   again.** 2 of 4. *"…he always is."* · *"Mara's still on about a loose
   stone…"*
5. **Someone did a thing once.** 2 of 4. Real, but heard as a pattern if
   overused.

**Stock a writer reaches for**

- Every event pinned to an invented place name, nearly always a crossing.
  4 of 4. A real person says "at the ford".
- A small money grievance: a toll charged twice, a copper bet. The same toll
  story in three mouths.
- "Half the night", "didn't sleep much", "not a flicker", "the plain way"
  (the reader said 17 lines; I count 15).
- One remark per reply, always as its last sentence.

### What the three runs agree on

- **A name, one thing that happened, then stop** was found by me in the first
  run, by the reader in the second, and again in the blind run. It is the
  safest shape here.
- **How things have been since**, **tied to last night**, and **always /
  still / again** each turned up without any seeding.
- ***They'd have had this*** did not hold up blind: its one use was flagged
  as written.
- **Placement matters more than wording.** In all three runs the asides that
  failed were mostly well-worded lines said at the wrong moment: in place of
  an answer, or a death after a routine question. The ones that passed hung
  off something that had just been said.

The first agent was asked for all forty play-throughs in one file. It ran out

## The trial: Sella with and without her remembrances — 10 October

Built after Phil chose that a memory is called up by what was just said
(`src/cards/sellaLife.js`; a memory is marked with what it hangs off, and is
added straight after the answer that names it; never in her first reply,
never in place of an answer, never on top of the explanation or her Need).
The same thirty seeds were played both ways:
`logs/thirty-calls-with-her-life.txt`, `…-without-her-life.txt`, and a copy
with the memory lines marked `[M]`. 387 tests pass; lint clean.

**Independent read of both.** 38 memory lines in 319 of her lines.

- 25 land · 7 hang loose · 5 repeat something she had already said in that
  call · 1 at the wrong moment · 0 badly worded.
- Its verdict: she reads **more like a person with them, but only modestly**.
  Best: *"Four goes at it. Les would've known what to do."*
- **The pool is too small.** Eight fixed sentences, word for word each time.
  "My old partner runs her own party now" is in 9 of 30 calls; the fuller
  form with Les never appeared. Les is mentioned in four calls and never
  introduced.
- **The repeats are a clash with the scenario's own text:** "He came to us
  in the spring" after the unmarked "Pell only came in with us in the
  spring" in the same call.
- **Hanging by a word is not enough.** "The party's. My old partner runs her
  own party now." is joined only by the word *party*.

**The larger finding is about the lines under them.** Of her lines that are
not memories, the reader failed about a third:

- *"I've been at this since last night thinking I was doing something
  stupid."* (her Need) is tacked onto unrelated answers in 28 of 30 calls.
- Her "own way" replies — *"What I do know is, …"*, and her baggage line —
  answer nothing asked. 21 lines open with a stock pivot.
- Two answers laid end to end: *"I am. I set it up myself. Nothing at all.
  It just sits there."*
- Stiff, uncontracted lines from the scenario's older text: *"I have given
  it a night and a morning. It has not settled."*

### What this cost, in model use

Today's delegated runs, by the token counts the agents reported: about 1.2
million on the mid-sized model (fourteen play-through agents, plus one that
saved nothing) and about 0.36 million on the largest (four independent
reads). That is separate from the main session, which is the larger share.

### After fixing the three faults the trial read found — no agents used

Counted directly on the same thirty seeds, before and after:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Calls where her Need line is said | 28 of 30 | 10 of 30 |
| Replies that answer nothing asked | 63 of 321 | 11 of 295 |
| Replies opening with a stock pivot | 21 | 0 |
| Calls saying "in the spring" twice | 5 | 0 |

- **Her Need** now surfaces only from a question she took up, and an oblique
  trigger fires one time in ten.
- **Leaving a question unanswered** now takes cause (the call has soured, or
  has started to and everything asked is something she guards), and what she
  says then is her baggage, once. Unasked statements about the problem are
  gone.
- **A memory hangs off the subject she was asked about**, not any word in
  her answer. "The party's. My old partner runs her own party now." can no
  longer happen.
- The memory about Pell joining is left off this call; the scenario already
  has her say it.

**Not re-read independently.** These counts are mine. The independent read
above describes the build before these fixes.

### Still wrong, known

- **The call is now always winnable by one fixed line of play.** A scripted
  agent that asks what happens, how she is going about it and what she was
  looking for, then offers the remedy, resolves 1,000 of 1,000 calls in four
  turns. Before these fixes it was 783 of 1,000. Her leaving a question
  unanswered was the only thing that ever blocked that path, and it is now
  rare. This is the *Solved* pathology in `docs/trust-and-verify-cards.md`.
- Eight memories, each said one way per fullness. The pool is small and the
  fuller forms are rare.
- Les can be mentioned in a call where the partner is not.
- Two short answers given together still read as fragments.
- Stiff lines in the scenario's older text ("I have given it a night and a
  morning. It has not settled.").
- The angle (Verify, Validate, Expand, Contract) still has no effect on what
  she chooses.
