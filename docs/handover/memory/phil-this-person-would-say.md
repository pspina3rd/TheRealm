Phil, 20 November, correcting my test of "would a person say it and be
understood at once":

*"There are two things you are linking together here that are actually
separate checks. Whether a person would say this. AND Be understood at
once."*

*"Whether a person would say it, is inaccurate to what we've been
discussing and attempting to build; 'Would A person say this?' which
requires a follow-up if/when true, 'Would THIS person say this?'."*

*"Be understood at once, is subjective. At once by whom? … More
accurately the question is, 'Does this specific person believe they will
be understood, if this is what is said?'"*

**How to apply:** judge every line of a customer's against two separate
questions, both about her, not about people in general:
1. **Would this person say this?** Her sheet, what she knows, how she
   sees things, her words. "A person could say it" is only the first
   step.
2. **Does this person believe she will be understood, saying this?** From
   where she stands, with what she thinks the listener knows. Not whether
   a general audience would follow it.
A line can be fine English and still fail either.
Related: [[phil-verify-by-reading-output]], [[phil-correct-or-not]].

**How to read, Phil, 20 November:** *"When reviewing the text, it is vital
to be considering it as a whole, and as smaller chunks: paragraphs,
sentences, and phrases and phrasings, and look at colloquialisms, turns of
phrase, metaphors, similes, etc. too. Who is this person speaking, what
does their character sheet tell us, what else do we know about this
person, what else can we discover about them, what else can we expect from
them, what would be unexpected for them?"*

So a review is: the whole call read through as one conversation (does it
repeat, loop, or read as mechanical?), then each answer, sentence and
phrase, then the colloquialisms and figures of speech, each held against
who she is: her sheet, her history in the record, what she knows, how she
talks, and what would be in or out of character for her.


**Characters are not built around a request.** Phil, 23 November: the
customer-service reason is the variable a character reacts to, and could
be any in the game; *"This isn't an enemy in a video game that only has one
area and one move to memorize."* Build the person first (sheet, sharing,
baggage, voice, notebook); requests are a separate pool handed to any of
them.
