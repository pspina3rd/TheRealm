Settled with Phil across 2026-09-08/09. TheRealm's core loop is **a
prisoner's dilemma with information**, and it is *cooperative*: nobody
wants a bad outcome. Alignment is the shared goal; human nature is the
obstacle.

**What crosses between the two sides is information**, across five
links, each a possible (not certain) failure point:
1. how well the customer states the request
2. how well the agent says what they need to make progress
3. how well the customer comprehends the request
4. how well the customer communicates the answer
5. how well the agent comprehends it and says what happens next

*What happens next* is what drives progress. Before the call even
starts, the customer has usually chased the merchant they bought from,
adding earlier links. **One broken link is enough to turn a routine
call into an unfamiliar one — and whether it's hard depends on the
agent.** Not every call has a fault; a clean call is ordinary, not
empty.

**Memory is unreliable by TYPE, not by decay** (Phil, citing research):
gist very high, emotion high, one's own words moderate-high, exact
dialogue extremely low (~10% verbatim after five minutes), personal
details low. So "you told me Tuesday" is structurally always suspect
and never a lie. Details are unreliable (you can't search precisely)
but gist is reliable (they catch you when you read back the wrong
record) — the two halves do opposite jobs.

**The soft skills are the mechanic**: *validate with the customer, then
search*; *paraphrase for comprehension, then act*. Searching straight
off what the customer said is the beginner's move. When a raw search
fails it opens new doors that hide the one you came in through. Verify
lines never make the customer the grammatical subject — "let me
confirm I…", "these results don't look right", "something doesn't seem
right". No blame; human error and Murphy's law are real.

**Every customer is a full RPG character with their own sheet** —
knowledge, emotions, baggage — pulled and loaded each time they call.
They grow and change, so what worked before may work better, worse, or
the same. The player's handling **permanently alters other characters**
(notably their knowledge), and an educated customer is harder to fob
off. Effects have *durations*: some last one round, some carry to the
next call, some both — decided by that customer in that moment.
**What persists is what was felt** (emotion buffers memory), and the
player cannot tell at the time which they have just done.

**How a caller shares information is a space, not a spectrum** — at
least five independent axes: volume, signal, certainty, accuracy,
openness. Certainty and accuracy do not move together. None of it
tracks how they feel about you. This produces people who read like
archetypes yet break from expectation — so **archetype is the player's
prior, never an input to the roll**.

Some callers want a person, not an answer — often subconsciously, and
they may never know it. It can sometimes be told and never confirmed,
because the only witness is unreliable on the question. So: no flag, no
readout; it surfaces later as whether they call back.
