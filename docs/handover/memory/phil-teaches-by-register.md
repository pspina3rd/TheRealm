Phil switches register constantly: concept, context, metaphor,
illustration, example. **Do not take every statement literally.** When
he says something like "a customer standing at a Node", the concept may
be the point and the staging merely illustrative.

**Why:** Taking illustrations as specifications produced a repeated
failure loop — build the literal detail, get corrected on the detail,
then discard the concept it was illustrating along with it. He named
it: "You take whatever I say literally, regardless of whether it is
concept, context, metaphoric, illustrative."

**How to apply:** Ask "is that a spec or an illustration?" before
building. That is *Trust and Verify* aimed at him rather than at a
customer. Also watch for two related habits he calls out: collapsing
his multi-axis models into scalars or binaries ("you walk up to a
spectrum, then pull back"), and swinging between extremes instead of
finding the variable ("like watching a pendulum"). He also sets
deliberate traps to see whether Claude will echo-chamber its own
argument — agreeing enthusiastically is not the same as being right.

**The trigger is formatting.** The flattening happens at the moment of
turning his words into a table, a fixed scale, or a list with one label
per item. On 2026-09-19 he illustrated a range in words ("available,
possible, probable, improbable, impossible") and it was immediately
turned into a ratings column — after the lesson was already in this
file. His words: "no matter the choice I make it gets flattened, then
we spend hours rebuilding it back… like teaching a student who knows
better, yet chooses not to learn." Knowing the rule did not stop it.

**Concrete rule:** when he describes a range, reflect it back in words.
Do not convert it into a table, scale, or one-label-per-item list
unless he asks for one. If about to assign a single label to each item,
stop — that is the flattening happening.

His training principles, which apply to working with him as much as to
the game: Actively Seek Knowledge · Trust and Verify · people don't
know what they know · people don't know what they don't know · slow is
smooth, smooth is fast · **moving forwards is not always progress**.
