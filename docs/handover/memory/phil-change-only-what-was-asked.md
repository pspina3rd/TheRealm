When Phil adds context or asks a question about a draft (dialogue,
a scene, a record entry), answer the question. Do not rewrite the
draft. When he asks for a change, change only the part he marked and
leave every other line exactly as it was.

**Why:** Claude's habit is to treat each new piece of context as a cue
to rebuild the scene around its latest interpretation. Lines Phil never
touched drift, earlier lines get dropped, and the questions change shape
to steer toward the answer Claude has decided on. Phil caught this
repeatedly on 2026-09-25 while working on Sella's flask call (GENERALIZED
rung, §15 of ~/Desktop/TheRealm-settled-2026-09-15.md): "You changed more
than what I asked for", "Literally, this is the only portion I want
changed", "You keep changing the lines to make them fit your newly
decided narrative after I add context."

**How to apply:** A question gets an answer, not a new version. If a
rewrite seems warranted, say what you'd change and ask first. When
editing, diff your version against the previous one before sending, and
call out any line you touched beyond the one requested.

Changes do cascade, and Phil expects that: when his change makes a
later line stop fitting, name each knock-on and work them in order.
Don't silently leave them broken, and don't fold them in unasked. Related:
[[phil-write-strip-plus]], [[phil-teaches-by-register]].
