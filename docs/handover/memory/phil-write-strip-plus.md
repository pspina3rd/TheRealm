Phil's editing method, the one he teaches new support agents. Use it on
anything written for him, and on customer-facing copy in TheRealm:

1. **Write it out.** That is the first draft, nothing more.
2. **Strip out the fluff.** Reduce to verifiable facts and actions
   only. Anything that is neither a fact that can be checked nor an
   action being taken comes out.
3. **Plus it back up.** Add context only where it is actually needed,
   add warmth (the soft skills), read once more, and decide whether it
   needs another pass. Act accordingly.

**Why:** Claude's drafts go from step 1 straight to step 3 — writing,
then decorating. The fluff is never removed, and context gets layered
on top of it, which is how the "dense wall of text that says nothing in
plain simple English" happens. Step 2 is the one being skipped.

**Replies too, not just drafts — and it's a medium, not a minimum.**
On 2026-09-26 Phil objected to replies that dumped everything at once,
then, after a two-line reply, to one "so short on context it's
impossible to comprehend." Swinging between the two is the pendulum
from [[phil-teaches-by-register]]. Aim for: one item per message; what
it is, in plain words, enough that it makes sense without scrolling
back; what's wrong with it; what I'd do; one question. Write it as
prose, not a list of lists of lists. And weigh every reply against the
whole conversation — what's been settled, rejected and pinned — not
just his last message.

**How to apply:** Strip first, then earn each word back. Step 3 is
*where needed*, not everywhere. Also avoid regionalisms and invented
words — Phil pulled up "twee" and "writeable down" in one paragraph.
