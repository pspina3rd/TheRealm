# Mana Crystals — second-half scene treatment

**Working story proposal; dialogue is drafted separately, not implemented.**
Read with [the approved structure](STORY_ARC.md),
[character foundations](CHARACTERS_DRAFT.md), and
[the regional outline](STORY_OUTLINE_DRAFT.md). The team's
[script at fe5c3e4](https://github.com/pspina3rd/TheRealm/blob/fe5c3e49473297c008b2167f8476c043db735d0f/docs/STORY.md)
supplies the existing scenes referenced below. Its first half remains intact.
The [dialogue draft](DIALOGUE_DRAFT.md) develops these scenes. The team's
[encounter appendix](https://github.com/pspina3rd/TheRealm/blob/d14b3c4f6bb173986e05af4ce89e9895c437a34b/docs/ENCOUNTER_DESIGN.md)
provides Irdrath, the seven singers of the Lishal, and the Malificat. The
Malificat name is treated there as decided; second-half mechanics remain
non-binding. Earlier Warden and Cantor placeholders are superseded.
All new action, motives, supporting roles, and supernatural rules here
are proposals, including the working identification of the ward's old man
as Fennick and the person he helped raise as Vesper. This treatment does
not establish biological parenthood.

These are selected dramatic scenes within substantial playable regions,
not a sequence of consecutive cutscenes. Exploration, ordinary encounters,
trade, rest, and practical work separate the major confrontations. Progress
percentages locate story beats, not timers or exact content measurements.

## Working decisions

- Preserve the Wyrm sleeping alive, the returned moonlight, Brightwater's
  stirring sleepers, Vale's gratitude, and the voice and footprints at the ward.
- Let the hero discover Vesper's next choice before it becomes irreversible.
  Vesper receives an intelligible opportunity to stop and chooses otherwise.
- Fennick's proposed personal connection complicates their decisions; the
  rite still works as a communal undertaking rather than a family privilege.
- Give the team's named underground residents continuing lives. Proposed
  roles: **Odile**, a waterkeeper, wants to move her refuge's residents to
  safer housing. **Ketter**, a bellwright and lampkeeper, wants someone else trained well enough
  to take a shift so they can leave their workshop without abandoning people.
- For a complete ending to evaluate, this version proposes that Vesper
  survives defeat without the borrowed power. Survival is not forgiveness.
  Their fate is a draft choice, not an addition to the approved brief.

## 01 · The victory that happened — 50%, existing scene

**Want and pressure:** the hero expects the Wyrm's defeat to end the crisis.
The beast slackens, the speaking stops, and moonlight returns. Give the
existing fall and chapter celebration room to land before the ward interrupts.

**Action and turn:** preserve the team's scenes without adding an explanation
of the animal's guilt or innocence. The new voice, soaked cloak, footprints,
and old man's recognition overturn the hero's conclusion, not the gains
the player has just witnessed. Relief becomes alarm and an immediate question:
who crossed the ward, and what can still happen?

**Consequence:** investigation begins at Sevenstones. There is no corpse,
secret fissure, or second Wyrm phase. This is the midpoint Ordeal and false
victory; it does not reset every village to its opening condition.

## 02 · Fennick stays at the threshold — 50–52%

**Want and pressure:** the hero wants a route after the footprints. Fennick
wants to go after their maker personally. Vale needs to keep the watch from
following into an unknown passage. Everyone has the voice and tracks as
evidence; none yet understands the lower network.

**Action and choice:** Fennick recognizes the ward's service markings but
cannot open its main barrier safely. The hero helps expose a narrow inspection
entrance. Fennick starts to prepare to descend, then entrusts the investigation
to the hero and stays to keep the entrance usable. In the proposed relationship,
they identify Vesper and admit their connection now, rather than withholding
it to manufacture a later surprise.

**Turn and consequence:** Fennick's urgency becomes reluctant trust. The
hero gains a responsible contact and an agreed place to bring evidence,
not a promise that Fennick already knows how to defeat the voice.

## 03 · Odile's packed belongings — 52–56%, Sunken Archive

**Want and pressure:** the hero wants the lower route. Odile wants the refuge
ready to leave: blankets are packed, but the flooded lift cannot carry its
sleeping residents. She knows the drains; she has no reason to believe
another fighter will stay once shown a shortcut.

**Action and choice:** she first offers directions that avoid the refuge's
work. The hero notices the lift problem and helps drain its landing before
asking for access again. Odile puts down her own packed bag and joins the
repair. Her reluctance comes from managing a departure, not a test of worthiness.

**Turn and consequence:** suspicion becomes a limited partnership. The
player earns access to storage records and a practical connection to the
Cistern Marches. Odile remains responsible for evacuation rather than joining
the hero's adventure as a reward.

## 04 · Irdrath knows every chamber — 56–60%, Cistern Marches

**Want and pressure:** Odile needs water and a safe transfer for the refuge.
Irdrath wants the sleeping chambers kept beside his heat. His ash body and
smoking hands register changes through the pipes; he releases a dangerous
pressure joint before Odile reaches it. The Firstcoal glows inside him.

**Action and choice:** he allows water for the sleepers but too little for
the people carrying their beds. Odile presents an independent drain that
can be tested with the sleeping chambers isolated. Irdrath follows the
plan and witnesses a safe trial. Heat remains steady, but he still seals
the exit in hardening ash: he cannot extend his care along their road.

**Turn and consequence:** respect becomes unease. The dispute survives the
correction of the engineering concern. Odile has blankets, braziers, and
people waiting above; Irdrath refuses to trust them. The transfer is prepared
openly. There is no invented human office or past explaining his authority.

## 05 · An order Odile refuses — Irdrath, Boss 2, about 62%

**Want and pressure:** Odile wants the transfer to begin through the tested
channel. Irdrath wants the beds kept beside the Firstcoal. The hero knows
which chambers are isolated and has witnessed the safe trial flow.

**Action and choice:** Odile calls the carriers forward despite Irdrath's
order. He hardens ash across the opening and surges toward the catch.
The hero intervenes. The team's ash, smoke, and buried Firstcoal identity
shapes the confrontation; exact phases remain encounter-design work.
Freeing the coal disrupts his control without removing it as a trophy or
breaking the walls protecting the sleepers.

**Turn and consequence:** after defeat, diminished smoke gathers around the
Firstcoal. Irdrath still worries about the cold. Odile hears the concern,
tucks a blanket around a sleeper's feet, and asks him to clear the door.
He does. The first stretcher passes before the hero leaves. This is a
limited surrender of control, not a speech of repentance or instant forgiveness.

## 06 · Vesper reads the same page — 63–67%, Rootways

**Want and pressure:** Vesper wants the recovered records, convinced they can
understand and use what the hero has found. The hero wants to know where
the voice is going. Both have reasons to keep the other talking; neither
knows the whole system.

**Action and choice:** Vesper spots an error in the hero's reading of a
diagram, providing useful knowledge. Another record challenges Vesper's
claim to be the voice's uniquely informed servant: earlier vessels were
promised lasting power too. Their interest sharpens before pride returns.
They take the route information they need and decline the hero's invitation
to compare the rest together.

**Turn and consequence:** mutual curiosity becomes rivalry. The records
propose a history older than the Wyrm; they do not suddenly explain the
Master's entire origin. A repaired transport line carries the hero onward.

## 07 · Ketter's lamp — 67–70%, Glass Warrens

**Want and pressure:** the hero needs to reach Vesper's relay access. Ketter
needs the lift lowered to a damaged landing. Their call repeats from empty
passages, but their moving lamp and damaged cable locate a real person.

**Action and choice:** the hero secures the frayed lift before lowering it,
losing time in the pursuit of Vesper. Ketter climbs aboard and retrieves
the lamp they use to signal carriers on distant landings. They show the
hero the different marks on the voice branch and the lamp feed. A carrier
starts a loaded lift at Ketter's signal; the player sees why the distinction matters.

**Turn and consequence:** haste becomes attention. Ketter's knowledge gives
the hero a specific way to stop the voice without stranding those carriers.
The rescue introduces the bellwright before their workshop in the next
region, rather than creating a disposable guide who vanishes after giving instructions.

## 08 · Vesper keeps their hand on the control — duel, about 72%

**Want and pressure:** the hero wants the relay isolated. Vesper wants to
control a power they now know has used other vessels. They understand the
risk and believe understanding gives them an advantage over their predecessors.

**Action and choice:** the hero arrives with a working isolation method,
not an accusation about Vesper's secret feelings. Vesper examines it; there
is a genuine pause in which cooperation is possible. They instead bar
access and attack when the hero proceeds. Fight Vesper using their own
body and skills, without the final form appearing early.

**Turn and consequence:** intellectual rivalry becomes personal defeat.
The hero secures the upper control and preserves Ketter's lamp feed rather
than chasing the retreating Vesper. The central relay below remains active.
Vesper leaves alive, angry at being outmatched and still able to choose
what happens next. Attitude changes texture, not whether the duel occurs.

## 09 · Ketter cannot leave the bell — 72–76%, Bell Hollows

**Want and pressure:** the hero needs the central relay's location. Ketter
needs a relief operator: the bells guide workers who are still below.
Their meal has gone cold on the bench; departure preparations keep getting
interrupted by another signal.

**Action and choice:** the hero helps restore a local signalling circuit
that ordinary workers can operate. Ketter resists handing it over, catches
themself correcting a competent operator, and finally lets that person finish.
They then give the hero maintenance plans and Fennick's correspondence.

**Turn and consequence:** irritation becomes the unfamiliar relief of being
able to leave. The letters show the surface stations and a missing lower return, connecting
the network to the unfinished rite. The hero carries that incomplete diagram onward.
Ketter will later help reopen the ascent; their return follows their own
new freedom, not a sudden obligation to the protagonist.

## 10 · The Lishal receives a refusal — 77–79%, Quiet Foundry

**Want and pressure:** the seven singers want the settlement to accept
permanent sleep. The hero wants access to the relay. Ketter wants to retrieve
tools left for repair and leave with the workers. The Lishal's meals,
bandages, and bedding provide real care before their coercion appears.

**Action and choice:** a worker understands the sleep offered and refuses it.
One singer argues that the worker should be allowed to take their coat.
Others close the exits, block a passage with bedding, and lower the shutter.
The hero and Ketter force the loading door. A worker is injured; Ketter
leaves the recovered tool case wedged under the door to carry them out.

**Turn and consequence:** hospitality becomes confinement. The dissenting
singer deliberately rejoins the frame; another reaches toward the injured
worker, then returns to the crystals. All seven contribute to the renewed
signal. Their individual acts carry responsibility rather than hiding it
inside one architect's speech. Unlike Irdrath's defensive hoarding, this
collective is deliberately extending sleep to new people.

## 11 · Vesper makes the offer — 79–81%

**Want and pressure:** Vesper wants access to the power the hero is about
to shut away. The voice wants a mobile vessel. The hero reaches the relay's
inspection gallery while trying to disconnect its coercive branch and
sees Vesper across the chamber.

**Action and choice:** the voice offers continued access in exchange for
embodiment. Vesper has seen the records and lost the duel; ignorance is
not the explanation. The hero copies the lower-return connections from an inspection-rail diagram
into the gap in Fennick's letter before opening a safe footbridge. When a
loading shutter begins to trap the escaping workers, the hero holds its
brake rather than crossing. Vesper sees the open retreat and declines it.
The hero's rescue choice delays pursuit while Vesper accepts the voice.

**Turn and consequence:** wounded pride becomes a deliberate gamble.
An observable change in Vesper's movement and voice confirms acceptance
before the relay closes. They depart through the mapped service ascent
toward Sevenstones. There is no additional battle here.

## 12 · The work continues without permission — the Lishal, Boss 3, about 82%

**Want and pressure:** the Lishal want to complete the broadcast. The hero
wants to stop it while workers keep essential lift signals running. The
rite's lower-return procedure has already been copied from the inspection
diagram into Fennick's letter; it is not a convenient ritual scroll dropped as loot.

**Action and choice:** disconnect the compulsory-sleep branch while the
local signal bell keeps answering. One singer observes that the rooms are
empty; another points to the people above. The hesitant singer fits the
manual drive. All seven resume their places at the differently coloured
crystals and attack the hero defending the disconnected branch.

**Turn and consequence:** their collective authority breaks when people
continue skilled work without their orders. Victory disconnects the sounding
frame while leaving evacuation signals intact. No automatic repentance follows.
Vesper already carries the voice beyond the machine's reach. The hero returns
with the copied procedure; Boss 3 is neither Vesper's second duel nor an
additional Master fight.

## 13 · People use the road — 82–85%, ascent

**Want and pressure:** the hero wants to catch Vesper. Odile's group wants
to bring the sleepers through the damaged ascent. Ketter knows how to lift
the obstruction but needs help holding the load.

**Action and choice:** the hero stays for the lift repair rather than racing
ahead alone. Odile helps direct traffic; Ketter trusts others with the control
while working under the raised frame. Their earlier choices now have visible
consequences. After the transfer, they keep the route running for those behind.

**Turn and consequence:** urgency becomes shared effort without disappearing.
News from the surface reports the violet stone dimming again as Vesper
approaches it. This proposed renewed drain explains the Road Back's dark
stones without undoing the earlier return of moonlight or Maren's cleared lake.
The hero reaches Sevenstones with help, evidence, and a route others can use.

## 14 · Fennick hears what Vesper chose — 85–88%

**Want and pressure:** Fennick wants evidence that Vesper can be brought
back. The hero wants preparations started immediately. Each holds information
the other lacks: Fennick knows the surface practice; the hero witnessed
the offered exit and Vesper's acceptance.

**Action and choice:** Fennick initially searches the account for a compulsion
that would excuse Vesper's decision. The hero describes only witnessed actions.
Fennick stops trying to turn that account into reassurance and examines the
recovered procedure. They agree to containment even though it may limit
their own chance to reach Vesper.

**Turn and consequence:** hope becomes grief mixed with resolve. Care for
Vesper remains; protecting the gathered people takes precedence. Under the
proposed relationship this is personal recognition, not a reveal of kinship.
The rite cannot extract a willing host's choices or kill the host remotely.

## 15 · The places people left — 88–92%, return tasks

**Want and pressure:** the hero needs material and volunteers. The people
asked have homes, supplies, and unfinished work. Play a small set of visits
through ordinary responsibilities, rather than making everyone praise the hero.

- Rowan needs a reliable account of Hareguy before discussing the rite.
  This draft keeps Hareguy among those still asleep; do not imply the team's
  Brightwater scene established that every sleeper woke. Rowan can contribute
  knowledge and preparation from Mossvale without walking a road his knees cannot.
- Bean divides a limited harvest between local care and a volunteered bundle.
  Ordor arranges transport on stated terms. Their contributions preserve
  generosity and livelihood instead of emptying both businesses for a fetch quest.
- At Sevenstones, use the mentors' existing Road Back rows at their relighting
  moments. Let their familiar actions carry the emotion: a steadied lamp,
  measured moonlight, hands at the stone. Preserve the team's written lines.

**Turn and consequence:** asking feels more exposing than issuing instructions.
Supplies and helpers are secured, but nobody promises that bravery guarantees
success. Optional relationships enrich these scenes; basic materials and
trained local helpers must remain available for any class and quest history.

## 16 · Someone catches Fennick's mistake — 92–95%, rite

**Want and pressure:** Fennick wants every preparation checked personally.
The hero wants to enter the inner ward chamber where Vesper has begun drawing
on the stones. The helpers need a plan they can carry out without waiting
for Fennick at every station.

**Action and choice:** during preparation, a helper spots a misaligned frame
Fennick has checked twice. Fennick accepts the correction and delegates the
outer stations. The recovered procedure depicts the wickless brass lantern;
Fennick sets it within the outer ring. Test the connections with a small
pulse of pale light before committing people. That checks the sequence,
not whether a living voice will be contained. The latter remains supported
by the records until Vesper's presence tests it. The hero waits for the
preparations despite Vesper's increasing pressure.

**Turn and consequence:** Fennick's control gives way to trust; the hero's
haste becomes deliberate readiness. Activate the boundary with helpers outside
the threatened inner chamber and a clear retreat for them. The procedure is
intended to prevent the
voice moving to another vessel; it does not weaken Vesper into a ceremonial
opponent or restore the remaining sleepers in advance.

Proposed procedure, established through the recovered evidence: the boundary
holds the voice within the inner ward, while the battle must break its active
bond to Vesper. People can leave after that separation; the voice cannot.
Fennick explains those separate responsibilities during preparation, before
anyone commits, rather than revealing a new rule after the fight.

## 17 · Vesper recognizes the hero — 95–100%, the Malificat

**Want and pressure:** Vesper wants the hero to recognize their new power
as vindication. The hero wants to end the drain on the stones and prevent
another escape. The voice wants a new route once the tested boundary blocks it.

**Action and choice:** show Vesper noticing the same habits they noticed in
the earlier duel. Their attention and bitter curiosity remain recognizable
before the voice intrudes. When the boundary holds, they understand the
cost of their bargain. They still choose to fight for control rather than
let the hero end the drain. This is their evolved final form, the Malificat, and the last boss.

Late in the battle, the appendix's Resurrection beat brings spectral chains
and desaturation. Fennick remains at the lantern rather than leaving the
station to reach Vesper. They use the source phrase, "Ves, en."; no meaning
or translation is invented. Assigning the line to Fennick remains this draft's
proposal. The lantern creates room for the hero to continue, not an automatic
victory. Exact phases and the appendix's six-row clearance remain tentative;
visual chains never change controls, and the current landing and skyline
remain visible even if upcoming previews are fogged. The final elemental
moment uses the source's mentor-or-alignment fallback for any class.

**Turn and consequence:** confidence becomes recognition of a trap, then
defiance. The hero wins the battle; the rite prevents the voice repeating
the Wyrm escape. Proposed resolution: the link is severed, the voice contained,
and Vesper survives exhausted and without its power. No separate Master fight
follows, and Fennick does not deliver an instruction to finish Vesper off.

## 18 · Getting someone home — aftermath

**Want and pressure:** Fennick wants to approach Vesper. The watch needs to
secure the chamber. The hero needs to know what happened to the remaining
sleepers. Relief does not settle responsibility for the harm.

**Action and choice:** Fennick waits while the watch makes the room safe,
then helps the injured Vesper without demanding reconciliation. Vesper has
no power left with which to bargain and offers no immediate conversion
speech. Their later accountability remains work for the realm, not a reward
the hero can waive on everyone else's behalf.
Once the people have left, the inner ward is sealed with the disembodied
voice contained inside it. The temporary battle boundary becomes the
completed containment; nobody is left standing beside an unsecured threat.

Return to concrete lives: Hareguy wakes and Rowan receives reliable news
before their reunion; Odile reaches safer housing with the refuge; Ketter gets
to leave a shift in someone else's hands. Pip is still concerned with his
tower and tomorrow. Show waking through several households and an interval
of recovery, not a claim that every loss was undone in an instant.

**Turn and consequence:** victory becomes relief people can act on. The
Return with the Elixir is the restoration of ordinary possibilities. Full
campaign completion belongs after the final form; the Wyrm remains the
midpoint achievement that made the rest possible.

## Review alongside the dialogue draft

The sequence carries the monomyth through the midpoint Ordeal, discovery
and cost, the Road Back, the final Resurrection test, and the Return with
the Elixir. Those terms guide structure; characters need not speak them.

Check the proposed Fennick identification, roles for Odile and Ketter, Vesper's
acceptance and surviving fate, the ward passage, renewed stone drain, and
rite mechanics as story choices. None has been promoted to approved canon.
The Wyrm's independent agency is still unresolved; these new scenes neither
assign it dialogue nor rely on an answer.

In [DIALOGUE_DRAFT.md](DIALOGUE_DRAFT.md), each exchange should pursue the immediate wants
above, using what that speaker actually knows. Let emotions appear through
attention, interruption, hesitation, practical help, and refusal. Preserve
the existing first-half and mentor lines; do not retrofit them to announce
the new plot. This treatment changes documentation only.
