# What the voice is: three further options

**Option paper · 4 November 2026 · author-only spoilers · planning document, no game changes**

The author held decision SQR-D02 (see [AUTHOR_DECISIONS.md](AUTHOR_DECISIONS.md)). Neither the
eighth stone nor the Long Dream from [Plan B](PLAN_B_SECRET_HISTORY.md) is approved yet. This paper
offers three more candidates, all different from each other and from those two. Each one answers
the question left open by finding F1 of the [story quality review](../reviews/STORY_QUALITY_REVIEW.md):
**what is the voice, why does it want people asleep, and how do the stones, the moonlight, the Moon
Wyrm and the sleep connect?**

Passage IDs (`SR-…`) refer to the [Story Reader](../WORLD_BIBLE_STORY_READER.md). Check and
question IDs (`WB-C…`, `WB-Q…`) refer to the [World Bible](../WORLD_BIBLE.md). Nothing here is
canon until the author picks it.

## Assumptions

1. **Rowan's well contributes but is not the only cause** (SQR-D03, approved). Every option below
   gives the crisis at least two other causes.
2. **No invented language.** No option needs "Ves, en." (WB-Q07, PB-D02). Each one suggests a plain
   English line for the lantern moment. Old words, where they appear, are ordinary English.
3. **The approved structure stays.** Wyrm at the midpoint, alive and asleep; Boss 2, the Vesper
   duel, Boss 3, the rite and the final form, in that order (WB-C08, WB-C10).
4. **Plan C's "drowsy" crystals are the likely sleep mechanic** on the board. Each option gives
   them a meaning, but none of them depends on it.
5. **The existing lines should become true.** A player who rereads the visions after the ending
   should find that the voice framed things its own way but did not lie (Plan B's WB-C20 idea).
6. **An old order is optional.** Plan B's Lamplighters were designed around the eighth stone and
   are held (PB-D01, -03, -04, -09). Each option says whether an order fits and what Irdrath and the
   Lishal become.

- People light the lane lamps **at dawn**, not at dusk (`SR-opening-04`).
- The well water is violet (`SR-opening-05`). Rowan "cracked the bedrock" with the old rune stone
  forty years ago, and "something breathed down there" (`SR-refusal-04`, `SR-npc-elder.confide.warm`).
- The visions begin only once the hero carries that stone.
- Selene: "Something deep below drinks the moonlight" (`SR-mentor-selene.motive`).
- The Wyrm coils around the moon-crystal. When it falls, moonlight pours up through the stone, the
  violet stone glows, and Brightwater's sleepers stir (`SR-wyrmFall-03`, `SR-chapterEnd-01`).

## Option 3 — The Lone Weaver

**For a child.** Long ago, magic was taught in seven schools, and every school began at the stone
circle. One brilliant student learned to braid two schools together, then three, then more. They
decided that if one person could hold all seven at once, nobody would ever be cold, hungry or hurt
again. They tried it alone. It was far too much for one body. The body was gone, but the wanting
stayed, sunk into the rock under the circle. The seven teachers of that time set their stones in a
ring around it. Now that wanting is the voice, and it is still looking for hands.

**The deeper layer.** This is a story about doing great things alone versus doing them together.
The voice is not a monster from outside the world. It is the best student magic ever had, who could
not bear to share the work. Vesper is following the same road, step for step. The hero's whole
journey, learning from seven different people and helping each of them first, is the answer to the
Weaver's mistake. The question the ending asks is not "who is strongest?" but "who will let others
help?"

**What it wants and gains.** It wants to finish its weave: to hold all seven schools in a living
body that it can steer. Every person carries a small, unguarded spark of each school while they
sleep (warmth, breath, movement). The Weaver draws loose threads from sleepers, the way you might
pull threads from a coat nobody is wearing. Each thread makes its reach longer. Sleepers are not
eaten; they are unravelled a little, and they cannot wake while a thread is out.

**Violet first.** The violet stone (Arcane, the school that acts on other minds) is the knot that
ties the ring together. It is fed by moonlight. The Weaver set the Wyrm around the moon-crystal to
starve the knot, because a loosened knot lets threads slip through.

**The Wyrm and Vesper.** The Wyrm is an old animal it put to sleep a century ago and threaded
through, a voice and a body with no will of its own: "a good throat". Vesper is what it has always
wanted, a waking apprentice who chooses. "The stones will answer you" is true: a bearer can draw on
all seven. "The Master" is the name the Weaver let Vesper give the Wyrm. "Malificat" is the old
teachers' word for a person holding all seven alone, which is what the Weaver once became.

**Why the ring holds.** The ring is a standing Weaving, made by seven people and kept by many. A
weave held by many hands outweighs one held by a single pair. When a waking helper keeps each
stone's school going, the bearer cannot draw on it, and the Weaver's thread cannot cross.

**Rowan's well.** Rowan's rune stone was one of the teachers' footing stones that pin the ring's
deep edge. When he cracked the bedrock with it, a single thread slipped free and found the Wyrm.
The other causes: the schools stopped visiting the circle generations ago, so nobody renewed the
weave; and the Wyrm's coil starved the violet stone.

**An old order?** Not needed. The ring's builders are simply "the first seven teachers"; today's
mentors are their heirs in teaching, not by blood, and none of them knows the story beyond a saying
("Every school begins at the stones"). Fennick can stay a caretaker at the circle with no order
behind him. **The Lishal** are a mirror of the seven teachers: seven carers who believe the
Weaver's finished weave would end suffering, and whose sounding frame pulls sleepers' threads down
to it. **Irdrath** needs no history: the Firstcoal is the Weaver's fire thread, lent to him. It
gives him warmth to keep and a grip on the chambers. Freed, it returns to the red stone.

**Gameplay.** This is the strongest fit. The seven schools are seven strands of one weave. Custom
Weaving is the Weaver's own art, and it unlocks after the Wyrm because the knot stone is lit again.
Drowsy crystals are loose threads; touching a crystal of the same colour ties it back. The final
rite is literally a Weaving the player builds while helpers hold the other stations.

**Canon kept or changed.** Keeps WB-C08, WB-C10, WB-C14 (the light test still proves only the
connections; the records supply the reason) and WB-C15 (helpers cover absent schools). It settles
WB-Q02 and changes WB-C09: the Wyrm had no choice. It adds a founding story for the schools, which
touches the WB section 5 rule against added histories for the mentors' institutions, though not
their private lives. The lantern line can be "Let go. We've got it." (WB-Q07).

**First-hour clues.** (1) A child's counting rhyme at the dawn lamps: "Seven to hold and one
alone; one alone goes into stone." (2) Rowan's stone shows seven braided lines with one loose end.
(3) The first lesson of any school ends with the same line: "Every school begins at the stones."

**Reveal.** In the Rootways, Vesper and the hero find that the first entry in the vessel record is
not a vessel. It is written in the first person: "I held all seven. I needed no one." At the final
form, the Malificat's shape matches the figure in the archive drawing.

**Strengths.** Very clear theme; the sharpest possible mirror for Vesper; explains why there are
seven schools, seven mentors and Weaving. **Weaknesses.** A founding myth for the schools is a big
addition; the voice becomes a person, so players will ask for its name and face. **Tone risk.**
Low to moderate. "Unravelled" sleepers can sound frightening; keep it to "a thread is out" and show
threads going home when people wake.

## Option 4 — The Fallen Moon

**For a child.** Long ago, a small piece of the moon broke off and fell. It struck the hills at
the place still called Moonfall, sank through the rock and came to rest deep under the middle of
the land. It is cold and dark down there, and it wants to go home to the sky. People found it and
built the stone circle above it. Each month they gave it a share of moonlight through the violet
stone, so it would not be lonely or cold. Then, little by little, it stopped waiting for its share.
It started taking.

**The deeper layer.** This is a story about homesickness turning into taking. The voice is not
cruel, but its longing is so big that other people's lives look small beside it. The visions'
grand lines come from something enormous, old and lonely, and that changes how they sound. Vesper,
who "once walked in the sun", hears in it their own wish to be somewhere else. The ending asks
whether you can care for something without giving it everything it wants.

**What it wants and gains.** It wants to rise. To climb, it needs light and a body that can walk
up out of the ground. Folk say that dreams float up to the moon at night and come back in the
morning. In this option that is true. The fallen piece catches dreams on their way up and keeps
them, each one a rung on its ladder home. A sleeper is not harmed, but they cannot wake until their
dream comes back.

**Violet first.** The violet stone is the moon's stone, the one that gives the fallen piece its
monthly share. When the voice began drinking all the moonlight, it drank the violet stone dry first.

**The Wyrm and Vesper.** The Wyrm is a moon creature that lived on moonlight in the caves. Drawn to
the fallen piece like a moth to a lamp, it coiled around the moon-crystal (a natural lens where
moonlight gathers in the rock). The voice turned its body into a straw: everything the crystal
gathered ran through the Wyrm and down. "I drank the moonlight" becomes true, through the Wyrm's
throat. Vesper is the walking body it needs. If it rose inside a bearer, it would take every caught
dream with it, and those sleepers would never wake. That is the stake. Vesper's "Master" is the
Wyrm they could see; the voice let them believe it.

**Why the ring holds.** The fallen piece is made of night light. Seven colours of daylight lit
together make a ring it cannot cross, the way a shadow cannot stand in the middle of a lamp. The
light test fits WB-C14: it shows the ring is lit, but only the records explain why night light
cannot pass it.

**Rowan's well.** Rowan's crack opened a shaft from the sky straight down toward the fallen piece.
For the first time in centuries, moonlight reached it directly. It woke hungry, and the well water
turned violet with its light. Other causes: a long run of thin moons that Selene has been measuring;
and the Wyrm, already drawn to the crystal, which the voice could now reach.

**An old order?** It fits lightly as **the moon-watchers** of Moonfall, who saw the piece fall,
followed it and built the circle. The tower and Ivo's moon rune become their observatory with no
map change (close to PB-D09). No warden order is needed. **The Lishal** are seven dream-keepers who
believe the fallen piece deserves to go home and that a sleeper's dream is a fair price ("nobody is
hurt"). **Irdrath** keeps the chambers warm because a moonless sleep is cold. The Firstcoal can
be the watchers' ember of the first sunrise after the fall, kept to warm sleepers.

**Gameplay.** Good. Drowsy crystals are dreams held down; same-colour contact returns them. The
seven schools are the seven daylight colours, and the rite order can be sunrise order, ending on
violet ("The violet stone went first, and it will go last", `SR-campaignRite-08`). Arcane opening
after the Wyrm follows naturally from the moonlight's return. The rite-as-Weaving reads as "building
a dawn by hand".

**Canon kept or changed.** This option keeps the most existing text. "Drinks the moonlight" and "I
drank the moonlight" become true, and "the moon above is just my pale reflection" becomes the
voice's own lonely view. The WB section 4 wording "claims to consume moonlight" is upheld rather
than retired. It keeps WB-C08 and WB-C10. It settles WB-Q02 and WB-C09: the Wyrm was lured, not
willing. It extends the Moonfall place lore. The proposed ending (WB-Q06) can stay, with one added
beat: the circle gives the moon its share again. The lantern line can be "Morning. Let them go
home."

**First-hour clues.** (1) Pip's task: "Mum says dreams float up to the moon and come back in the
morning." (2) Rowan at the well: "Cold as moonlight in a bucket." (3) Selene's letter, delivered at
the refusal: "There's less moonlight than my tables say, and what's missing isn't going up. It's
going down."

**Reveal.** At the lair, the player sees the light running into the Wyrm and down, not up. When it
falls, light rises (midpoint confirmation). Underground, the archive holds the watchers' drawing of
a falling star, with the circle built over the place where it sank, captioned "The moon's share".

**Strengths.** Gentle and memorable for children; makes the Moon Caves, Moonfall and Selene matter;
reuses the existing visions. **Weaknesses.** A sympathetic villain may make players ask why the
ending contains it rather than sends it home; that needs a clear answer (it would take the dreams).
It explains the schools less directly than Option 3. **Tone risk.** Low. Dream-stealing could worry
a few young players at bedtime; show caught dreams as kept safe and returned.

## Option 5 — The Night Watch

**For a child.** When the stone circle was built, its makers gave it a guardian: a watcher made of
the stones' own light. Every night it turned the lamps down, settled the storms and kept sleepers
safe. Every dawn, the lamplighters lit the lamps to tell it the night was over, and it let everyone
wake. Then the makers were gone, and nobody remembered why the lamps were lit at dawn. The watcher
kept watching. It saw every fall, fever, flood and lost crew. In the end it decided the safest
thing was for nobody to wake up at all.

**The deeper layer.** This is a story about love that will not let go. The proposed second half is
already about care turning into control: Irdrath will not release the sleepers, the Lishal will not
accept a refusal, and Ketter cannot hand over a shift. This option makes the voice the largest
example of that pattern. Its lines sound like a tired guardian: "Sleep." "Rest instead." It is
frightening because it is sincere. Fennick, who has to let Vesper choose, and Rowan, who has to
trust someone else with his son, are the human answers.

**What it wants and gains.** It wants everyone kept safe, which to it means asleep. It has no body,
so it can act only through sleepers. Each sleeper it keeps becomes another place it can see and
speak from, and it is stronger for every one. It wants a waking body so it can walk the surface and
put everyone to bed.

**Violet first.** The violet stone is the morning stone, the one that wakes the mind each dawn,
and it is fed by moonlight overnight. To stop mornings, the watch had the Wyrm coil around the
moon-crystal, so the violet stone could no longer charge.

**The Wyrm and Vesper.** The Wyrm is the biggest sleeper it ever kept, a living post on the watch:
"a good throat, old and very obedient". "I have others" means every sleeper, which is why the
threat continues after the Wyrm. Vesper heard it as a child by the stones; it was the first thing
that seemed to watch over them alone. "The stones will answer you" is true, because the watch was
made from the stones' light. Vesper's "Master" is the Wyrm, the only body they ever
saw it use.

**Why the ring holds.** The ring does not just cage it; it gives it orders. The makers built the
watch to stand down when all seven stones are lit by waking hands, because that was the signal for
dawn. Inside a fully lit ring it cannot leave its post. That also explains why a tended stone
resists the Malificat. The final rite is a deliberate dawn, and the ending can let the watch itself
rest for the first time.

**Rowan's well.** Rowan cracked one of the watch's deep listening stones. Through his shaft it
heard a village's troubles directly, for the first time in a hundred years: aching knees, sick
children, fear. It confirmed everything it feared. The other causes: the dawn lamp custom had faded
to a habit nobody understood, so the "morning" signal had been weak for generations; and the
Wyrm's coil.

**An old order?** This is the natural home for Plan B's **Lamplighters**: their job was literally
to light the lamps that tell the watch it is morning, and the wickless brass lantern was theirs.
**The Lishal** were the order's seven night singers, who sang sleepers calm. They came to agree
with the watch. **Irdrath** was set to keep the chambers warm "until morning", and he is the same
fear in a smaller form. If the author prefers fewer added histories, Irdrath can simply be a
cistern keeper who shares the fear, and the order can stay unnamed.

**Gameplay.** Good. Drowsy crystals are lamps the watch has turned down; same-colour contact is a
small morning. The seven schools are the seven things that must happen for morning (warmth, water
moving, ground holding, wind, storms breaking, growth, a clear mind), so they need no founding
myth. The rite is a Weaving in dawn order, with helpers keeping each stone lit. Weaving is less
explained here than in Option 3.

**Canon kept or changed.** Keeps WB-C08, WB-C10 and WB-C15. WB-C14 holds: the light test shows
connections; the makers' record supplies the stand-down rule. It settles WB-Q02 and WB-C09 (the
Wyrm was kept, not willing) and answers WB-Q04 and WB-Q05 directly. It knowingly waives the WB
section 5 rules for Irdrath and the Lishal if the order is used (as PB-D03 and PB-D04 did). It
turns the existing dawn-lamp line into the core clue. The lantern line becomes plain English: "It's
morning. You can rest now." It is said to the watch, not to Vesper.

**First-hour clues.** (1) At the dawn lamps (`SR-opening-04`), Bean: "Dawn, not dusk. Always has
been. Tells the stones it's morning." (2) Inspecting a shuttered house: "Someone has tucked him
in. The lamp is turned down. The door was barred from inside." (3) Vision 1 shows concern rather
than scorn: "You'll hurt yourself patching wells. That old man's knees. Let him rest."

**Reveal.** In the archive, Odile's records include the makers' duty list for the watch. It ends
"…and at dawn, wake them." Irdrath later says "until morning" in the same words. At the rite,
Fennick's drawing labels the centre "the Watch", with the rule: "When all seven are lit by waking
hands, the Watch stands down."

**Strengths.** Unifies every second-half conflict under one theme; gives the gentlest and most
moving ending; uses a clue already on screen in the first minute; gives the Lamplighters a reason
to exist. **Weaknesses.** It is closest in shape to the eighth stone (an order, a ring and sleep),
which the author has held; a voice that means well can blunt the danger unless the coercion is
shown plainly. **Tone risk.** Low for fear, but moderate for theme: a guardian who keeps children
asleep "for their own good" must be clearly wrong without making real carers seem suspicious.

## Recommendation

**Choose Option 5, the Night Watch, with one element borrowed from Option 3.**

It gives the voice a motive a child can say in one sentence ("it wants to keep everyone safe, so it
won't let anyone wake"). It ties together every proposed second-half conflict, from Irdrath and the
Lishal to Ketter's shift and Vesper's choice, instead of adding a new idea beside them. Its ending,
a deliberate dawn and a guardian finally allowed to rest, is the most literary of the five and
still suitable for young players. Its best clue, lighting the lamps at dawn, is already in the first
scene of the game. It also replaces "Ves, en." with a plain line that matters: "It's morning. You
can rest now."

Borrow Option 3's reason for the rite: a weave held by many hands outweighs one held alone. In
Option 5 that is why the dawn signal needs waking helpers at every station, and it gives custom
Weaving a story reason without adding a founding myth.

**Choose Option 4 instead** if the author wants the lowest writing cost and the lightest touch on
canon. It keeps the most existing text, makes Moonfall and Selene central, and is the easiest
for children to picture. **Choose Option 3** if Vesper's mirror and the meaning of Weaving matter
more than tone.

Whichever is chosen, the next step is the same as in Plan B: a one-sentence truth table, a clue
ledger with three plants per reveal (one guaranteed, one in the first hour) and a guaranteed
confirmation, and the matching World Bible updates for WB-Q02, WB-C09 and WB-Q07.
