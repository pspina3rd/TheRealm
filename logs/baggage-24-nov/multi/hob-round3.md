# Hob Tallis, round 3

## Tracking

**Experience:** I sent one of the company's blades out for work I couldn't do at my own forge, and it hasn't come back when I expected it.

### Profile A

**How their baggage sits with them:** Having been told before that he was mistaken when he wasn't, he gives the plain facts once and expects to be doubted before he is heard.

**Their response:** "One of the company's blades went out for repair and hasn't come back. I need to know where it is." [20]

### Profile B

**How their baggage sits with them:** Straight answers from them in the past mean he says what happened and leaves the rest to them, without padding it.

**Their response:** "I'm ringing for the company. A blade I sent out for repair should be back by now. Can you tell me where it's got to?" [25]

### Profile B, knowing a lot worked out

**What they know going in:** He knows it travelled on the company's account with a paper from the carrier, and thinks it is most likely sitting at the depot. He would not think to have a reference number to hand.

**How their baggage sits with them:** A smith who likes work done right takes good past service as the way things are meant to go, so he offers his own guess at the cause and asks them to confirm it.

**Their response:** "I'm ringing for the company. A blade I sent out for repair should be back by now. It'll be sitting at the depot, I expect. Can you check?" [28]

### Mix C

**How their baggage sits with them:** Having once been told he was wrong when he wasn't, he comes with his facts in order, and good dealings with others make him precise rather than hostile.

**Their response:** "I'm ringing for the company. A blade I sent out for repair is late coming back, and I've got the paper from when it went. Can you look it up?" [30]

### Mix D

**How their baggage sits with them:** Good past dealings with them make him trust the job will be done, but feeling overlooked keeps him to the one thing he wants, and not knowing how it works he does not guess what they will need.

**Their response:** "I've got a blade that went out for repair and hasn't come back. Can you find it for me?" [19]

## Access

**Experience:** A blade of the company's was held for collection, and the person there wouldn't hand it to me because it isn't on my name.

### Profile A

**How their baggage sits with them:** Being refused at the counter is what he already expected, given he has been told he was wrong before when he wasn't, so he says only what happened and asks who can do it.

**Their response:** "They wouldn't let me take a blade of the company's. I'm the one the company sends. Who can take it?" [20]

### Profile B

**How their baggage sits with them:** Used to things going smoothly with them, he takes the refusal as a slip to be put right and asks for the fix directly.

**Their response:** "A blade of the company's is waiting for collection and they wouldn't give it to me. Can you put that right so I can take it?" [26]

### Profile B, knowing a lot worked out

**What they know going in:** He knows the account is the company's and not his, and thinks there is a list of people allowed to collect that can simply be added to. He would not think the company's officer has to agree to it first.

**How their baggage sits with them:** A man who pays what he owes and expects the same trusts that a plain request, correctly put, will be met.

**Their response:** "I'm the company's contact, but they wouldn't hand me a blade at collection. Can you add me to the list of people who can take it?" [26]

### Mix C

**How their baggage sits with them:** Braced to be told he is mistaken, he gives the refusal exactly as it was said, and having been served well elsewhere he expects there is a proper way through.

**Their response:** "A blade of the company's was held at collection. I was told I'm not on the account. What do you need to put me on it?" [26]

### Mix D

**How their baggage sits with them:** Good past service makes him trust they will explain, feeling overlooked keeps him short, and not knowing how it works he asks why rather than what to do.

**Their response:** "They wouldn't give me one of the company's blades at collection. Can you tell me why?" [16]
