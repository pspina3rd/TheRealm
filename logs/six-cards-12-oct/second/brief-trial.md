## What to do

The agent asks you a question. Your mind goes to one **aspect** of it — a
person (who), a thing (what), a time (when), a place (where), a reason (why),
or a way of doing it (how) — and you reply from there. The aspect is given
on each line and is often not the one the question asked about.

This is how it sounds when it works. Asked about a payment, whatever the
question was, a person whose mind goes to *who* says one of:

> I paid it. · He paid it. · We paid it. · No one paid it.

Short, plain, and the agent can tell she heard him.

**How to write a reply**

- Say it the way you would actually say it out loud to a stranger on the
  phone. Most replies are under ten words. Many are two or three.
- It has to be a reply. The agent must be able to tell you heard the
  question, even though you answered from somewhere else.
- Use only what is in *What you know* and *Your life*. Invent nothing. "No
  one", "never", "nowhere", "nothing" are real replies where they are true.
- Do not open with "Because" unless you were asked why.
- Do not hand the question's own words back as padding ("How are you going
  about it?" — "I've been going about it since…").
- No filler: "right there", "same as ever", "same as always", "that's
  where", "that's when", "simple as that", "plain as day".
- Then give the **one key word** in your reply that belongs to the aspect.

**When there is no natural reply, say so.** If a real person's mind would not
go to that aspect on that question — if anything you could write would sound
like she was not listening, or would be padding — write `CANNOT` as the
reply and leave the key word empty. This is a correct answer, not a failure,
and you should expect to use it. A forced reply is worse than `CANNOT`.
